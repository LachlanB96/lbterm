# Tab links

One tab's Claude session waiting on another's work, shown on the tab rail, so with twenty tabs open it's plain what waits on what (Lachlan's ask, 2026-10-29: an agent often has to pause while an agent in another tab finishes something, and with that many tabs he forgets which). A right-button drag of a tab's row onto another's makes the first wait on the second. A line joins their pads down the rail's left edge, and the waiting pad turns purple. The agents don't know about it: when the tab waited on finishes what the other waits for, the program that keeps the links tells the waiting session it may carry on, and if it stops again still blocked, its pad and line go red.

LBTerm draws the links and takes what Lachlan does to them. Deciding when a tab has finished, and telling the waiting one, is a program of the config's: on Lachlan's PC, Terminal's `shared/claude/links.ts`, which has the Haiku digest that keeps each session's objectives judge it, and sends the waiting session a message through Claude Code's SendMessage.

## Turning it on

```lua
config.lbterm_links = {
  file = [[C:\...\tab-links.json]],   -- the links, as the config's program keeps them
}
```

It needs `lbterm_tab_panel`, and `lbterm_summaries` for each session's label and objectives. Without it, a right press on a row opens the tab menu at once, as before.

## The file

Read off the GUI thread whenever its size or time changes (`wezterm-gui/src/state_files.rs`, beside the Conductor's state file), parsed by `lbterm_extras::links::read`:

```json
{ "links": [{ "waiter": "<session>", "on": "<session>", "what": "LBTerm to land the links", "objective": 2,
              "state": "waiting", "note": "" }],
  "waits": [{ "session": "<session>", "what": "the rail's lines drawn", "on": "<session>", "objective": 1, "dismissed": false }] }
```

Sessions are Claude session ids, the `claude_session` user var Terminal's claudehook sets in a pane. A link's `state` is `waiting` (the tab it's on hasn't finished), `sending` (it has; the waiting session is being told), `sent` (told), `stuck` (told, and it stopped again still blocked: `note` says why) or `failed` (it couldn't be told: `note` says why). `objective` is the objective of the session waited on that it waits for, from `<session>.objectives.json` (lbterm/OBJECTIVES.md). A wait is what a session says it waits for on another session, and, once the program has found it, the open session and objective that would end it: a suggested link. Anything else in the file is left alone, and a link or wait without its sessions, or with a state LBTerm doesn't know, is left out.

## What the rail shows

- **The waiting tab's row** (`lbterm_panel::row`, with `Tab::link`): once its program has handed the turn back, its pad is purple (`RowState::Linked`, `palette::LINKED`, the tab menu's purple a shade deeper so the number reads) and its words say "Waiting on Tab Wave: what it waits for". A tab that also asks Lachlan something (a question in text, a picker) keeps the purple with an orange ring round the pad (his 4a: "if it also has question, it gets the orange outline"). Working, a permission prompt and a login stay the program's own. Gone wrong, the pad is red: "Still blocked: why", "Couldn't tell it: why", or "Tab Wave closed before it finished" when the session waited on is in no tab of any window. Told, "Tab Wave is done, told it to carry on" until it works again. A row with several links shows the worst (`links::Standing`). The tab waited on keeps its own look.
- **The lines** (`links::lines`, `links::pieces`): down the rail's left edge, between the open tab's red bar and the pills (`Layout::link_lane`), from a hollow mark left of the waiting tab's pad to a solid one left of the pad it waits on. Purple while waiting, green once what it waits on has finished, red gone wrong. Each takes a lane no other line on its rows has, the shortest nearest the pills; at most three side by side (`LINK_LANES`), a fourth sharing the last. Each row draws its own pieces of the lines in its own slot, so they move with the rows when tabs come, go or change places, and a row whose pieces change dissolves like any other change to it (the pieces are in its look's hash).
- **A suggested link** (Lachlan's E7): a wait the program has matched to an open session, with no link yet and not dismissed, is drawn as a dashed line in the link purple at 55%. A left click on its dashes makes it (`confirm`); the tab menu can make or dismiss it too.
- **A line's card** (his E3): the pointer resting on a line for 300 ms opens a card beside the panel, level with where the pointer came onto the line: "Scroll strip waits on Tab Wave", where it stands in the line's colour ("Waiting", "Done, telling it to carry on: Lachlan marked it complete", "Done, told it to carry on", "Still blocked: why", "Couldn't tell it: why", or for a suggested line "Suggested: a click on the line makes it"), what it waits for, and the objective of the tab waited on that it waits for when that says more (`links::line_card`, `links::stands`). The closed panel stays closed while the pointer is on a line, so the rows and lines stay in sight beside the card; an open one stays open. Onto another line, that one's card at once; off them, it goes after 200 ms (`lbterm_panel::Hover`, the tasks card's rest and grace). A line under the pointer is its lane between its two ends, or across from the lane to either end's mark, with 2 px either side (`links::on_line`, `lbterm_link_line_at`), the first link's line the pointer is on. A connection's line is the same with an end at each member (Connections, below), and it's taken over a link's only when it's nearer the pointer (`links::line_distance`, the distance to its lane or to a piece across), the nearest connection's otherwise: the lanes are 3 px apart at 96 DPI with 3 px of slack either side, so a pointer on a group's lane, level with a row where the next lane's line has a mark, was on both, and the one found first opened its card (lbterm/PITFALLS.md, trap 50).

A line between two tabs needs both on this window's rail; a link to a tab in another window shows only on the waiting row's words.

## What Lachlan does

- **Make one:** a right press on a row, dragged (past `tabs::dragging`'s 6 px), then let go on another row whose tab has a Claude session: `toggle` fires, which makes the link, or takes away the one already there. While it drags, the tab panel still sees the pointer, so the rail opens under it as ever. A right press that doesn't move opens the tab menu when it's let go (it opened on the press before). Any other button while it drags puts it down. The drag is in `wezterm-gui/src/termwindow/tab_links.rs`.
- **The drag's cards** (his E5 and E6; `render/link_drag.rs`): a line from the dragged pad to the pointer and a ring round the row under it that it would wait on. Past the panel, level with the pointer, a card of what the dragged session says it waits for (`links::waits_card`), and beside that, level with the row under the pointer, a card of that tab's objectives, the unfinished first (`links::aims_card`). They sit beside the panel, not under the pointer, so the rows it's dragged over stay in sight. When the dragged session's wait matches one of that tab's objectives, the line and ring are green, both blocks are lit green, and with the compositor a light runs round each block, carried by DWM; let go there, the link waits for that objective.
- **The tab menu** (`lbterm_extras::menu`, its rows for links under a rule of their own): Stop waiting on each tab it waits on (`cancel`); "<what it waits for> is now complete by <the tab it waits on>" while it waits or went wrong (`release`, his E2, in his words: for when the tab waited on is done and its digest hasn't said so; "Its task" when the link names nothing), which tells the waiting session now, the same first line its message gets; Wait on it again where a link went wrong (`again`); and a suggested link's Wait on it, as suggested (`confirm`) and Dismiss the suggested link (`dismiss`). The menu widens past its 220 px to fit the longest of these rows, as far as the window lets it (`menu::layout`'s `words`).

Each fires the `lbterm-tab-link` Lua event, `(window, pane, link)`, `link` being `{action, waiter, on, objective}` (sessions by id; `on` and `objective` when they're known). LBTerm does nothing more itself: the config's handler changes the file. Until the file says the change, for up to 5 s (`PENDING`), the rail draws it already (a link made shows waiting, one taken away goes, a dismissed suggestion goes, one marked complete shows telling it, its line green), so the drop answers at once. Terminal's `lbterm.lua` runs `terminal links <action> <waiter> [<on>] [--objective N] --by lbterm`, and only for its own file: a test window's handler and file are its own.

## How it's drawn

- The lines and marks are drawn with each row in the rail's rows layer (z-layer 46 with the compositor, the body without), the marks again over the open panel's highlight. `Layout::link_lane`, `link_end`, `link_width`, `link_end_size`.
- The drag: the line and the ring in z-layer 88, the cards' shadow, card and words in 89 to 91, all shown by the compositor's `top` visual over the panel, as the tab menu's are. The light's template is z-layer 92 (`Target::LinkLamp`); `Compositor::prepare_link_lamps` shows it in a visual per matched block in `top`, its offsets laps of 90 points round the block every 1.4 s (the Conductor's acting lap), still with motion off. Without the compositor there's no light.
- A line's card is drawn in the drag's layers, 89 to 91, the drag's card look (`lbterm_link_card`), while no drag is under way.
- The state file (lbterm/STATE.md): each rail row's `link` and `asks`, its `state` `linked` and its `connected` (the connections made it's in), the rail's `links` (each line's rows, lane, look, lane's x and the ys of its two ends) and `connections` (each one's id, state, lane, member rows, lane's x, the ys of its marks and their x), `link_drag` (the row picked up, the row it's over, whether it matched, whether Shift makes it connect, the cards, their words and their matched blocks) and `line_card` (the link's line by its place, or the connection by its id, the card, its words).

## Connections

Two or more tabs can be connected, so their Claude sessions know they should be talking to each other (Lachlan's ask, 2026-11-01; his picks 1a to 5a and E6 to E8). Where a link is one tab waiting on another, a connection has no direction: its sessions are told about each other (label, project, messaging name, what each is working on) and message each other through Claude Code's SendMessage, and a line in each of their prompts keeps it in mind. The config's program (Terminal's `shared/claude/links.ts`) keeps them in the same file, finds the pairs that probably should be (both wrote the same file, or the objectives' digest judges that their work overlaps), sends the introductions and records the messages. LBTerm draws them and fires what Lachlan does to them as `lbterm-tab-link` events.

### In the file

Beside `links` and `waits`, parsed by `lbterm_extras::links::read` into `Links::connections`:

```json
"connections": [{ "id": "c-aaaaaaaa-bbbbbbbb-mh2x", "members": ["<session>", "<session>"], "state": "suggested",
  "at": 1793250000000, "stateAt": 1793250000000, "by": "files", "why": "both write lbterm/render/thumbnails.rs", "note": "",
  "messages": [{ "from": "<session>", "to": "<session>", "at": 1793250000000, "text": "..." }] }]
```

- `members`: two or more Claude session ids, each once (a session named twice counts once, and an empty one not at all). A suggestion has two; one that's made can have more (his E6: a third tab connected to a connected one joins it).
- `state`: `suggested` (found, not yet his), `connected` (his) or `declined` (he said no: read, but drawn nowhere). Any other state, fewer than two members, or no `id`, and the connection is left out, as a link without its sessions is.
- `by`: `files`, `digest`, `lbterm` or `cli`. `why`: why it was suggested, clipped to 120 characters (`WHY_CHARS`). `note`: empty, or why an introduction couldn't be delivered.
- `messages` (his E7): the last five (`MESSAGES`) its members sent each other, oldest first, each's `text` clipped to 160 characters (`MESSAGE_CHARS`); read only on a connected one.

### What Lachlan does

Each fires the `lbterm-tab-link` event, `(window, pane, link)`, with a `link` table of its own (`lbterm_extras::links::Act`), and the rail draws what was done until the file says it, for up to the links' 5 s (`PENDING`; `links::ConnectionChange`, laid over the file's word in `lbterm_links_pending`, beside the links' changes). A later change replaces an earlier one only where it says the same thing again (`ConnectionChange::replaces`): an accept or decline of the same connection, the same tab leaving the same connection, a Shift-drag between the same two tabs; two tabs leaving one connection both show until the file says each:

- **A left click on a suggested line** (its lane between its members' marks, or across to a mark: `links::on_line`): `{action = 'accept', connection = <id>}`. Drawn connected at once: steady, solid marks, no light. The pointer is a hand over a suggested line, as over a suggested link's.
- **Shift and a right-drag**: a row dragged (the links' drag, past `tabs::dragging`'s 6 px) and let go on another row whose tab has a Claude session, with Shift held as it's let go: `{action = 'connect', waiter = <the dragged session>, on = <the session dropped on>}`. The program toggles: it connects them (a new connection, or one joining the other's: his E6), or, where the two are connected together already, takes the dragged one out. Drawn at once by the same rule, worked out from the file as it stands when it's let go: together already, the dragged one goes from each connection they share (one left with fewer than two members goes); else it joins the connection made the tab it's dropped on is in, else that tab joins the dragged one's, else a suggestion between the two is made, else a new connection of the two shows (under `PENDING_ID`, `pending`, the rail's own id, until the file has it under the program's); and a suggestion between them goes once they're connected. While Shift is held the drag looks cyan (`palette::CONNECTED`: its line to the pointer and the ring round the tab under it), and its cards are both tabs' objectives (`links::aims_card`, the dragged one's past the panel level with the pointer, the other's beside it level with its row; in a window too narrow for two objectives cards side by side (the open panel, two cards 320 px wide at 96 DPI and the gaps: 946 px in tab-connections.ps1's window, its open panel ending at 294), the other's under the dragged one's, or over it where under runs out, `links::fits_beside` and `links::stacked_top`, rather than across it; a wait link's drag keeps its cards as they always were), nothing lit green: a connection matches no wait, so a wait that matches the tab under the pointer lights nothing. Shift is read live, from each key message and each pointer message (`termwindow/held.rs` hands the modifiers to `lbterm_link_drag_held`), so the look follows it with the pointer still; let go of Shift mid-drag and it's a wait link's drag again, as it always was, its match lit green and `toggle` fired if it's let go so. While the drag is under way the active pane's outline doesn't go green for Shift as well (his 2a, 2026-11-01): `termwindow/held.rs` leaves Shift out of the outline's colour while a tab is dragged (`links::outline_held`; the state file's `tint`, beside `held`), Ctrl and Alt still showing, and the drag over with Shift still held, the outline is green again. That's the drag once it's under way, past the 6 px: a right press that hasn't moved may still open the tab menu, and until it moves the outline shows Shift as ever. The drag's start and end each paint a frame, which takes the outline to white and back on `arrive_out` and `arrive_in`.
- **The tab menu**, under the links' rows, in the same section (`links::connection_rows`): for each connection made the tab is in, "Leave the connection with <the others' labels>" (`{action = 'leave', connection = <id>, session = <this tab's session>}`; drawn at once without it: the line shrinks to the others, or goes with one left); for each suggestion it's in, "Connect with <label>, as suggested" (`accept`) and "Decline the suggested connection with <label>" (`{action = 'decline', connection = <id>}`; it goes at once). A declined one has no rows, and nor has a connection the rail draws before the file has it (`PENDING_ID`, `Links::made_of`): its id is the rail's own, and a Leave would name it to the program (lbterm/PITFALLS.md, trap 52); its rows come once the file says it, under the program's id. The menu widens to fit the longest row, as for the links' (`menu::layout`'s `words`).

The config's handler runs `terminal links ...` on Lachlan's PC; a test window's handler writes each event to a file of its own (`perf/tab-connections.ps1`).

### The events

Everything Lachlan does to a link or a connection is one Lua event, `lbterm-tab-link`, `(window, pane, link)`, its `link` a table whose `action` says which and whose other fields are strings (sessions by id), `objective` a number. A handler tells them apart by `action`; a field not listed for it is absent. The links' are as they were before connections; the connections' are `lbterm_extras::links::Act` (`action()`, `fields()`).

| What he does | `action` | The rest of `link` |
|---|---|---|
| A right-drag let go on another row with a Claude session, Shift not held | `toggle` | `waiter` (the tab dragged), `on` (the tab dropped on), and `objective` when the dragged tab's wait names the tab dropped on and one of its objectives |
| A left click on a suggested link's dashes, or the menu's Wait on it, as suggested | `confirm` | `waiter`, `on`, and from a click `objective` when the wait names one |
| The menu's Dismiss the suggested link | `dismiss` | `waiter` |
| The menu's Stop waiting on | `cancel` | `waiter`, `on` |
| The menu's "<what> is now complete by <tab>" | `release` | `waiter`, `on` |
| The menu's Wait on it again | `again` | `waiter`, `on` |
| A left click on a suggested connection's line, or the menu's Connect with <label>, as suggested | `accept` | `connection` (its id) |
| The menu's Decline the suggested connection with <label> | `decline` | `connection` |
| The menu's Leave the connection with <the others> | `leave` | `connection`, `session` (the tab's own, the one leaving) |
| A right-drag let go on another row with a Claude session, Shift held as it's let go | `connect` | `waiter` (the tab dragged), `on` (the tab dropped on); the program toggles: connects them, or takes `waiter` out where they're connected together already |

The handler's program changes the file, and the rail draws what was done until the file says it, for up to 5 s (`PENDING`). LBTerm does nothing else with them.

### How it's drawn

- A connection's line and marks are pieces of its rows, as a link's are: drawn with each row in the rail's rows layer (z-layer 46 with the compositor, the body without; `render/tab_panel.rs`, `tab_panel_link_pieces`), in the lanes `links::lay_out` gives the connections once the links have theirs, and again over the open panel's highlight, each member mark rimming the next (`Lanes::rims`). Hollow marks while it's suggested, solid once made. Without the compositor a suggestion's line is dashed (`links::dashes`), carrying on from one row's slot to the next.
- Only rows the rail shows count (`Layout::row_visible`): a connection's line is under the pointer only with every member's row shown (`lbterm_link_line_at`), and a suggestion's light runs between the topmost and lowest of its members' rows shown, none with fewer than two (`render/connections.rs`). A branch that changes which rows the rail shows (feat/new-tab hides rows at its foot, `Layout::hidden`) changes both: whichever lands second checks them (lbterm/PITFALLS.md, traps 35 and 43).
