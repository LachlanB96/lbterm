# At rest

What LBTerm does while nothing happens: no thread waking on a timer, no frame and no file read, unless something it shows changed; and when something does change, it shows at once. Lachlan, 2026-10-29: "We want speeds that we can boast about", and "We should have checks that what we do uses the expected resources", after the Conductor's view was found burning 80% of a core at rest. He runs eight or more Claude Code sessions in tabs on LBTerm's mux domain all day, with the Conductor running, so whatever a resting window does, it does all day.

`perf/at-rest.ps1` holds it (below). This file says what each of the GUI's threads and timers does at rest, what was measured before and after the watchers stage of `perf/at-rest` (2026-10-30) its Conductor stage (the seat and the hub, the same evening), its transcripts stage (the scroll strip's conversations and the sessions' summaries, 2026-10-31) and the review's fixes after them (folders that go and come back, what Windows can't keep, the same morning), and what still wakes and whose it is.

## The Conductor's seat and hub

Lachlan's window has the Conductor's seat and hub on their pipes all day, and since this stage `perf/at-rest.ps1` plays both, on pipes of the test's own: the seat sends its view and conversation as LBTerm subscribes and answers its commands, and the hub hears where the sessions are. Nothing was wrong with them at rest itself: with nothing said, both threads sleep on their pipes, in every build. What they cost was what the seat says while nothing LBTerm shows changes, and each paint's bookkeeping.

- **The seat's lines repainted every window.** The seat sends its whole view whenever any of it changes, on a 250 ms timer after the change (seat.ts `changed`): any session's status, summary or the hub's guesses, an event, a question. While the Conductor writes a reply it sends the reply so far ten times a second. Each line repainted every LBTerm window, view open or not, though a closed view shows nothing of it: the pad's orange was the seat's count of asks, and nothing else of the seat's is drawn. Now what the seat says repaints the windows whose view is open (`lbterm_extras::seat::Views`) and, with none open, asks nothing of the GUI. The pad's asks are its state file's (`button.json`'s `asks`, which the seat writes at once as each ask comes and goes, SEAT.md's "Lifecycle", and the state-file watch follows), so an ask shows sooner too: the seat's view comes 250 ms after the change, the file at once. The subscription stays up with the view closed, for `seat.show`.
- **A seat that never answered kept threads.** Each command had a connection of its own, a thread blocked reading its reply and another watching the time; when the time ran out nothing ended the read, so a seat that kept the connection without answering (hung, not ended) kept the thread and its handle for as long as it ran. Now the one `lbterm-seat` thread writes commands on its subscription (SEAT.md lets a subscriber) and waits on the pipe, a command, a config reload and the next reply's deadline at once, through a pipe opened for overlapped I/O (`lbterm_extras::seat::link`): a reply that never comes is said at its deadline, and nothing is left waiting on it.
- **Each paint built the hub's panes line.** Every paint of the rail copied every window's Claude sessions, built the hub's `lbterm.panes` line and wrote it out, to find it the same as the last one sent. Now each window's sessions are compared with what it gave last before anything is built (`lbterm_extras::conductor::Panes`; its test paints a thousand frames with the sessions where they were and builds no line), their ids moved in, not copied. Each paint also cloned the Conductor's state file's fields up to four times (it's shared as an `Arc` now), the projects card's config and the objectives' settings (borrowed now), and the question colours looked up every pane's draft with none anywhere (they look at no pane now while no draft exists).

## The scroll strip and the summaries

Lachlan's Claude sessions reach transcripts of 30 MB and more, one of 5,800 rows, and the open tab is nearly always one of them, so what the scroll strip does with its conversation it does all day, on the GUI thread and on the thread that reads the transcript (`lbterm-conversations`). Each was measured against a transcript made up to that size, never a real one: lbterm-extras' long stand-in (`conversation/long.rs`, written the same way by `perf/conversation-cost.ps1`), 276 messages of a prompt, a fold of five file reads and a 14-line answer, 46 MB and 5,801 rows, as `perf/fake-claude.ps1 -Reads 5` draws it.

- **An append redid the whole conversation.** Each time the transcript grew, the reading thread built a new snapshot of all of it: every block's pointer copied, every mark made again and every line indexed again (a map of every line's first six letters, a string each), then compared the whole of it with the last snapshot under the lock each paint of the strip takes. The GUI laid every block out again for the new snapshot. Now the blocks, their marks and where to find each are kept in pieces of 128 blocks that snapshots share (`conversation/pieces.rs`): an append builds again only the piece it changed, a revision says there's something new instead of a compare, and the GUI lays out only from the first block that changed (`Snapshot::layout_from`, from `Chunks::first_difference`).
- **A look at the screen went through every block.** Where Claude Code's view is comes from the rows on screen, each matched to the blocks and lines it could be: every block's key was tried for each row, and every line sharing the row's first six letters. Now each row is found by the exact words a match must start with, which the matching rules allow (`pieces::Found`, a sorted list of hashes a piece), and held to the old passes over every block for every row of every screen the tests know (`pieces_tests`). And the screen was read again, at most every 120 ms with a repaint of its own to catch up, whenever anything in the pane changed, Claude Code's spinner included; now only when the conversation's rows or the input box's rules change, or the conversation or the pane's size does (the state file's `scroll_strip.looks` counts the reads).
- **The transcript was looked at while anything painted.** Every paint of the strip asked for its session's conversation, and the reading thread opened and looked at the transcript again every 500 ms of them, reading 32 MB a look: a long transcript opened in a tab where nothing else painted stayed part-read until something did (4,041 of the stand-in's 5,801 rows, in every run), and Claude's new words showed only at the next look. Now each window's strip says which session it shows, and the thread reads that one's transcript and permission records when Windows says they changed (`crate::watch`; a transcript not written yet is watched for through the projects' folder and every folder in it), from where it stopped, to the end. Only the windows showing the session repaint for it, where every window did. A session no strip shows is kept while a pane names it, so showing it again reads only what was added: the reading thread used to forget, as it went round for any session, those no strip had asked for in ten minutes, and read them again from their start.
- **Each summary look copied the summary.** The rail asks for every tab's summary on every frame (its label and folder, the tasks rows, the link labels, the objectives strip, the window strip's words), and each look cloned its words and every line of its detail under the store's lock. Now the store hands out an `Arc<Summary>` and each caller copies the one field it shows. And each change to any of a session's seven files read all seven again; the statusline writes `<sid>.cwd.txt` and `<sid>.tasks.json` as it goes, and now each change reads the file it was to.

## The review's fixes

An independent review of the branch (2026-10-31) reran the measurements above and got them, and found what they didn't test: a watched folder that goes. A folder removed and made again, or renamed away and a new one made, was never watched again, so the Conductor's pad never showed the new file (two runs, 6 s each), and nothing was in the log; the installed build's 250 ms look had shown both in 73 to 255 ms. And it doubted the scroll strip's "the spinner reads the screen 0 times", measured only against fake-claude. What changed:

- **A folder that goes is watched again.** notify's Windows backend took every completed read but a cancelled one for a buffer of changes: a folder removed under its watch (its read ends with `ERROR_ACCESS_DENIED`) was read again, which failed in silence, and a read that ran over (`ERROR_NOTIFY_ENUM_DIR`, or nothing written) was taken for an empty buffer. A folder renamed gives its own watch no word: the handle follows it to its new name. ## What still wakes, and whose

- **NVIDIA's D3D12 driver.** Two of `nvwgf2umx.dll`'s threads wake about 65 and 22 times a second in every build, frames or none, about 1.5 ms a second of CPU: most of what a resting LBTerm costs now. Not LBTerm's code. On the OpenGL front end one of `nvoglv64.dll`'s wakes ten times a second instead; with `lbterm_compositor = false` on WebGpu both drivers' threads wake about 97 times a second each. Whether something LBTerm holds (a fence waited on, a swapchain's frame latency object) makes the D3D12 driver look this often wasn't found out.
- **Busy folders.** Windows watches folders, not files. The Conductor's `button.json` shares `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Megaproject/conductor` with its seat's and hub's files, and Terminal's `tab-links.json` sits in `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Megaproject` itself, where a dozen projects write. Counted for a minute: 3.5 and 7.4 changes a second at 18:05 on 2026-10-30, 3.26 and 7.26 at about 07:10 on 2026-10-31 (the PC locked). This morning's were 49 files renamed into place in the Conductor's folder (seat.json 37 times, hub.json and cursors.json 6 each, button.json not at all), and in the machine state's about 51 files renamed into place and the times of folders in it changing as their files were written (conductor 98, warden 84, experience 18, chronos-jobs 14). A test window reading the real two files, two runs of 30 s on 2026-10-30: notify's thread woke 229 and 261 times (7.6 to 8.7 a second, about 0.35 ms a second of CPU), its neighbours' changes stopping there and lbterm-state-files not waking at all, where the 250 ms look woke that thread 4 times a second for 0.8 ms a second. (Before the watch passed on only what's about its files, the thread woke for every neighbour's change too, 283 times in 30 s, and for every folder on the way written in, 47.) `perf/at-rest.ps1` writes neighbours at this morning's rates since (its step 1d): 4.65 and 4.68 wakes of notify's threads a write, nothing else of LBTerm's waking and nothing painted. Moved into folders of their own by their writers, the watch would cost nothing at rest: InMail went to Conductor and to Terminal on 2026-10-31 asking for that, each to publish the new path under the name lbterm.lua reads it by (`@Conductor:button`, `@Terminal:tab-links`), so LBTerm needs no change. Terminal moved the tab links that day, to `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Megaproject/links/tab-links.json`: counted for a minute at 13:21 on 2026-11-01, that folder had no change while the machine state's had 5.12 a second, so their watch costs nothing at rest from a GUI's next config load. The Conductor moved its `button.json` too, to `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Megaproject/conductor/button/button.json`, alone in its folder so a watch on it wakes for nothing else (its contract's `@Conductor:button`, read on 2026-11-09, when it was there; not counted since), so lbterm.lua's `state_file`, which reads that name, follows it with no change here.
- **The mux server** looks at its socket's file every 3 s (fix/mux-socket-guard's `keep_socket`, 20 wakes a minute). The server's track.
- **A pane's programs.** Each pane's program runs in a job of its own (perf/key-latency, `mux/src/lbterm_procs.rs`), and the process's one `lbterm-jobs` thread wakes for each process that starts or ends in any pane: twice a program, so a Claude Code session's statusline and hooks wake it every few seconds a session, in the mux server for Lachlan's tabs. That's all it does for them: a walk of the PC's processes (`lbterm-procs`) comes only when what the pane shows changes, its foreground's name in a pane titled by it or the foreground's end in a pane that shows its name or folder (lbterm/PERF.md, finding 49). ConPTY sends such a pane's title again as each program ends, unchanged: locally an output notice and two tasks on the GUI thread a program, on a mux domain the server's main thread once and nothing pushed. `perf/at-rest.ps1`'s 1e measures both (2026-11-01: 10 programs in 21 s, 20 wakes of lbterm-jobs, no walk, no frame).
- **The Conductor's hub** says its guesses again every 45 s, which `lbterm-hub` reads (3 wakes for 2 lines in step 1c) and nothing else wakes for: the rail's frame for nothing at each old end is gone (above). While the hub is down LBTerm tries it again every 30 s at most, and the seat likewise (a command or a config reload tries the seat at once). Windows says nothing when a pipe is made. The files each writes as it starts (`hub.json`, `seat.json`) could be watched while it's down instead, but they share the Conductor's folder with the other's files (3.5 changes a second between them, above), so notify's thread would wake far more often than a try every 30 s does, and LBTerm's config would need their paths (the Conductor's contract publishes them, Terminal's `lbterm.lua` would pass them).
- **The Conductor's seat** sends a view whenever any of it changes and its reply ten times a second, with the view closed too, since LBTerm subscribes for `seat.show`. `lbterm-seat` wakes for each line and keeps what it says (181 lines: 260 wakes and 13 ms of CPU), and nothing else does. No InMail has gone to it about this yet.
- **The config's own watch** is upstream's (`config/src/lib.rs`): each file the config came from and each it adds to the reload list, and the config's folder unless that's the home folder. Lachlan's config is `~/.lbterm.lua`, a loader, so it's watched as a file, through his home folder: every change made directly in the home folder (`~/.claude.json`, which Claude Code writes, among them) wakes the config's notify thread, which drops what isn't about the loader. A test window with a config of its own, reloads on, saved ten times, two runs of each build at about 08:28 on 2026-10-31: the installed build had 40 handles more after them (four a save: a folder's handle and a semaphore for each of two watches), this branch none.
- **A folder further up renamed** than a watched folder's parent goes unseen: Windows tells the watch nothing, its handle following the folder to its new place, and only the folder's own name is watched in its parent. The next follow (a config reload, any change seen) watches the right folders again. Removing a folder further up has to remove the watched one first, which is seen.
- **The GUI thread** is woken by other programs' messages and by other GUIs' sweep of the GUI sockets (above); neither paints.
- **The scroll strip's watches** are through folders too: the shown session's transcript through its project's folder, where Lachlan's other sessions in that project write theirs, and its permission records (`<sid>.events.jsonl`) through the summaries' folder, which the summaries' own watch has too, so a second notify thread wakes for that folder's changes and lets the others go.
