# Compositor and motion

LBTerm draws through Windows' compositor so that panes can move. This file is the design: why it's built this way, how a frame reaches the screen, how motion is described, and what's done. The `lbterm-motion` skill (`.claude/skills/lbterm-motion/SKILL.md`) holds the rules for designing a new animation.

## Why the compositor

WezTerm draws everything (window background, every pane, the split lines, the tab bar) in one pass into one swapchain, on its one GUI thread. Animating inside that loop means every frame of motion needs that thread, and the thread blocks: `wezterm cli list` takes 200 to 290 ms with ~18 panes, and the summary pane's split arrives just as a Claude session starts, which is exactly when panes move.

With DirectComposition each pane is its own visual with its own swapchain, and DWM moves, clips and fades the visuals at the monitor's refresh rate (160 Hz on the main monitor) on its own thread. LBTerm only redraws a pane when its content changes. A stalled GUI thread freezes the text, not the motion.

DirectComposition rather than Windows.UI.Composition (the WinRT layer over it): it's plain Win32 COM with no DispatcherQueue in WezTerm's message loop, and wgpu 25 already creates swapchains for DComp visuals (`wgpu::SurfaceTargetUnsafe::CompositionVisual`). DComp has no springs, so LBTerm samples each spring itself and hands DWM the curve as cubic segments (`IDCompositionAnimation::AddCubic`). The motion on screen is then exactly the spec, and the sampling is unit-tested.

Rejected: animating inside the wgpu renderer (stalls with the GUI thread), and a WebView2 layer for CSS/JS (can't hold terminal text; two input systems; 100+ MB per window). CSS survives as the syntax for curves.

## How a frame reaches the screen

`config.lbterm_compositor = true` with `front_end = 'WebGpu'`. Otherwise LBTerm draws the way WezTerm does.

- The window is created with `WS_EX_NOREDIRECTIONBITMAP`, so DWM keeps no GDI surface for it. A DComp target on the HWND holds the visual tree. wgpu is pinned to DX12 (Vulkan can't present to a DComp visual).
- `WebGpuState` keeps the wgpu `Instance`, so it can make a surface per visual. Every surface shares one device and queue.
- The paint pass is unchanged except that it records which quads each pane emitted (`[start, end)` per sub-layer of z-layer 0). The scrollbar thumb is painted after the panes so it isn't counted as part of the active pane.
- `call_draw` runs the same vertex buffers once per surface, each with a projection that maps that surface's rectangle of the window onto its own swapchain, and a subset of the quad ranges. Pixels outside the surface fall off its edges, which is the clipping.
- Every visual with content of its own is a swapchain, and D3D12 lets one command list, and one `ExecuteCommandLists` call, write to at most 8 (`COMMAND_LIST_TOO_MANY_SWAPCHAIN_REFERENCES`, `..._QUEUE_...`). With the tab panel's five visuals, `under` and `over`, a tab's second pane made nine. So a frame records into one encoder per 8 visuals (`lbterm_motion::command_list_for`) and submits each on its own, and a frame that draws no visual submits nothing. Each visual drawn is one render pass, binding in turn every z-layer it has quads in (`lbterm_motion::Passes`; What a frame costs, below). Past the limit the list fails to close with `E_INVALIDARG`, and wgpu 25.0.2 then deadlocks in `Device::handle_hal_error` on the GUI thread; one submit past it removes the device instead.
- After presenting, LBTerm commits the visual tree only when it changed (a layout change, a new or closed pane, an animation).
- Each visual's frame carries its swapchain (`Frame::chain`), and the ones presented are handed to `present.rs` after the commit, which watches the one that shows each timed action until DXGI's frame statistics say it's on screen; the motion the frame starts is walked over the compositor clock's statistics once it settles (`Compositor::playing`; lbterm/PERF.md, "What's hard to time").
- Only what changed is drawn (below): a visual whose swapchain already shows what the frame would draw into it isn't acquired, drawn or presented.
- What a frame costs (below): templates made once for what they're made from, each layer's vertex buffer kept and written in part, bind groups kept, a render pass a visual, the motion's curves parsed once a config.
- Loops (below): the animations DWM plays for ever, which keep it composing every refresh, play only while the window can be seen.

While the tab panel is open, each pane's visual shows somewhere other than where its surface was drawn from: `PaneLayer` keeps the pane's rectangle (its surface and projection) and where it's `shown` (its offset and clip) apart, and the planner works on where panes are shown.

A pane is a card (`lbterm/DESIGN.md`, "Shape"; `render/card.rs`): the surface colour with 12 px corners, a gutter in from the edges of its room (the window, the tab bar, the tab panel's strip) and half a gutter short of the middle of a split's gap, so neighbouring cards are a gutter apart and nothing is drawn between them. The pane's visual is its card with half a gutter round it, so neighbouring visuals meet in the middle of the gap. The middle snaps to a pixel boundary the way the GPU fills quads (a pixel centre exactly on an edge belongs to the quad on its right or below, so `ceil(edge - 0.5)`, not `round`), the card's edges are whole pixels from there, so each visual holds exactly the pixels its quads fill and text stays 1:1. Each pane draws the window background first, which keeps its visual opaque to its corners.

**A card's edges ride on its visual.** With the compositor a card's edges are cut from template cards drawn into visuals of their own (when they change, as every visual is) and never shown, each drawn twice: as wide as the window and the template's height (its corners, top and bottom edges), and as tall (its left and right edges). The mask is the window background outside the card and clear inside; the line is the outline alone. Every pane shows nine pieces of the mask as children of its visual: the four corners, and the four edges cut to length, all sharing the templates' swapchains (`IDCompositionVisual::SetContent` with one swapchain on many visuals; `wgpu::hal`'s D3D12 surface hands it over). The top-left corner sits still; the right-hand pieces sit a fixed distance from the visual's width and the bottom row from its height, so each is driven by the pane's own motion of its clip, shifted (`compositor.rs`, `Edges`). The same samples, shifted, land the pieces where the clip is in every DWM frame, so a card stays whole and rounded at every size it passes through, while the content inside keeps its size and is cut off at its edge.

**One outline, on the pane with the keyboard** (`lbterm/DESIGN.md`, "Panes"). The outline is nine pieces of the line in a group of its own over the panes (`FocusOutline`), its offset and its pieces driven like a pane's. It follows the pane with the keyboard on whatever motion moves that pane (the same curve, from the same place, so the two move as one), and when the keyboard moves it glides to the next pane on `focus_move`, from wherever it is and keeping its speed; a tab switch places it on the next tab's pane at once (`lbterm_motion::focus`), and what of the old tab's outline it doesn't draw there fades out with that tab's panes (Motion, below). A pane without the keyboard is drawn undimmed, and its container is tinted to 85% (`palette::INACTIVE`) by a colour matrix toward the window background, going there and back on `dim` (`lbterm_motion::dim_tint`: each channel 85% of itself and 15% of the background's, the matrix's scale and offset on the level's curve, its alpha left alone), so upstream's `inactive_pane_hsb` is only for drawing without the compositor. That's what the pane at 85% opacity over `under` showed, in the stored values DWM composes in, so its card keeps the surface colour and its text sits at 85%, but the pane stays opaque. Until 2026-10-30 it was the container's opacity, and then 15% of whatever was under it came through: the window background at rest, but once its tab was left the next tab's panes, there at once in the frame of the switch, while the pane with the keyboard showed none of them (measured with two panes at `slowdown = 5`: 0.15 of the next tab against 0.00 in the first frame, the gap 0.15 × (1 − share) to the end; a switch back jumped by as much). Now both show the same share in every frame (`perf\tab-switch.ps1`, step 6: to 0.01 in each of 24 to 26 frames on the way, unlocked, 2026-10-30). In Lachlan's look (`motion\capture.ps1`, a tab split in two switched from at `slowdown = 5`) the dimmed pane fades on `tab_switch` from its first frame, 1.00, 0.97, 0.81 and on, 0.002 RMS (worst 0.007) over 35 frames, and interrupted 450 ms in it turns at 0.09 and comes back up 0.07 a frame. The dim itself is as it was: in `motion\focus-demo.ps1` at `slowdown = 5` the pane losing the keyboard goes from 255 to 220 and the one taking it from 220 to 255, each steadily, crossing at 238 and 236, and `motion\compare.ps1` finds the same 4,322 soft pixels as with the opacity. The effect is on the container only while the pane is dimmed or on its way back, so a pane with the keyboard at rest has none for DWM to run. The keyboard reaches the new pane at once: only the light moves. Without the compositor the pane with the keyboard draws its own outline and the others are dimmed by `inactive_pane_hsb`.

Things learned building it:

- Upstream hands WGSL its projection with `to_arrays_transposed()`. That only works because a window-centred orthographic projection has no translation. A pane's projection has one, and transposed it lands in `w`: the perspective divide then shreds the frame into horizontal streaks. The compositor's draw uses `to_arrays()`.
- The visible differences from drawing without the compositor: the panes without the keyboard, and the soft pixels at the edges of one visual over another. A pane without the keyboard is its visual tinted 85% of the way from the window background, as it would be at 85% over it, so its card keeps the surface colour and its text is at 85% (white to 220); without the compositor upstream's `inactive_pane_hsb` dims card and text alike, in linear light (at its default brightness 0.8 the card goes from `#121212` to `#0F0F0F`, white to 231). An edge's partly covered pixel (the soft pixel either side of the active outline, outside the rail's ring, either side of the open tab's ring and its bar, each a visual over another) comes out brighter with the compositor: without it the GPU blends that pixel in linear light; with it DWM lays the visual over the one under it in its stored, gamma-encoded values. Measured by `compare.ps1` (2026-10-30): at most 4 levels a channel at the outline, 10 at the rail's ring, 34 at the open tab's red ring over its pill. Inside a visual the GPU blends as it does without the compositor, so the rail ring's inner soft pixel, over the rail drawn in the same visual, comes out the same.

**Only what changed is drawn** (since 2026-10-30). WezTerm draws every quad of the window every frame, and with the compositor that was every visual's swapchain drawn and presented every frame: 24 of them in a window with four panes and the rail, about 10 ms of a 20 ms frame, whatever changed. The InMail button's count changing, which only the strip shows, drew all 24. Now each quad's vertices are folded into a number as they're written (`quad.rs`: every setter folds what it's given, and quads copied in whole fold their bytes; the GPU's upload memory is too slow to read back). A pane's cached lines, copied in every frame, bring hashes made once (`HeapQuadAllocator` keeps each quad's hash beside its vertices, folded by the setters as it's laid out, as a mapped quad's is; since perf/line-quads-by-content a kept line is laid out at row 0 and its hashes are moved with it to its row as it's copied in, `lbterm_motion::content::moved`, PITFALLS 129; until then they were made from boxed quads' fields the first time a line was applied, `content::Kept`; a heap's quads are written by the same setters in the order they're called, as a mapped quad's are, where main's `BoxedQuad` replayed its fields in a fixed order, corners and gradient last: PITFALLS 38 has where that differed and the call sites read for it): folding their bytes again each frame added about 1 ms to the paint pass of a window with four panes of coloured text, now at most 0.7 ms more than a build without any hashing with a spinner in one pane, and at most 0.8 ms with one printing at full speed, every line moving (medians, interleaved runs) (`HeapQuadAllocator::apply_to`, measured 2026-10-30). Before acquiring anything the frame works out each visual's key: the quads it would draw, in order, with the glyph atlas, the text's colour transform, its rectangle and its clear colour (`lbterm_motion::content`, `render/composited.rs`'s `lbterm_content`, the same `lbterm_ranges` the draw uses). A visual whose swapchain shows that key already (`Layer::shows`, the key of the frame last presented into it; nothing once the swapchain is made or resized, and the window background's beside `WebGpuState::surface`, reset when it's configured) isn't acquired, drawn or presented, and DWM goes on showing its last frame. Acquiring one without presenting it isn't an option: each acquire takes from the swapchain's frame-latency semaphore, which only a present gives back. A frame dump (`LBTERM_DUMP_FRAMES`) draws every visual. The state file says how many visuals each frame drew and kept, and which it drew (`visuals`, lbterm/STATE.md). An InMail change now draws the strip's visual and the swap's (2 or 3 of 24), and a key typed into one of four panes draws that pane's visual alone (1 of 23).

**What a frame costs** (since 2026-10-31). With only what changed drawn, what a frame still did was the work before and around it, the same whatever changed. Three things went:

- *Templates built quad by quad.* The paint pass is WezTerm's: it builds every quad of the window again each frame. Some z-layers hold templates that never change while the window doesn't: the card templates (a tall one as tall as the window, its sides in 8 px pieces for three bands, about 1,800 quads at 1,920 pixels), the Conductor's outlines and their light (about 900, while any outline shows), and the panel's glow, lit ring and running light, which with the compositor are drawn open and lit always (520, about 1,600 and 1,152). perf/lbterm-layers, beside this, made the panel's four into quads kept and copied into their layers every frame, each hashed already (render/kept_quads.rs), as the panel's body, rows, marker, words and progress still are, whose layers others write too; the cards' and the outlines' it left built every frame. Each template is now made only when its key changes (render/kept.rs: the window's size, as every quad is placed from its middle, its DPI, where the atlas's white is, the shape and quad generations that a reload, a new font or atlas, a lost GPU and a resize move, and what the template is made from: the background, the rail's layout, the pill an outline goes round, the light), and its layer keeps the quads otherwise: `RenderLayer::lbterm_keep` counts last frame's in again, hashes and all, and nothing is copied, hashed or written. One mechanism a layer: the panel's four are kept this way now, not copied in. A layer only one template writes into is kept; one that something else writes into isn't, and Glium, which turns through three buffers, never keeps one. An outline's side is one quad as far as its colour stays the same (`lbterm_stroke`), so the grey ring and the card templates' lines are a quad a side, and the lit ring's gradient keeps its 8 px pieces. The textures come out pixel for pixel as before in `LBTERM_DUMP_FRAMES`' six frames (every card template, every panel visual, the three ring templates).
- *Buffers made again.* Every frame made a new vertex buffer for every z-layer with a quad, mapped as it was made, which wgpu backs with a second buffer and copies the whole of across as it's unmapped, at the most that layer had ever held, and turned through three of them for nothing: 20 to 40 buffers and copies a frame with the compositor, whichever visuals it drew. Now each layer keeps one buffer (`VERTEX | COPY_DST`) and the paint pass writes its quads on this side (`WebGpuVertexBuffer`, its writes in termwindow/gpu_work.rs); the GPU's copy is written only in a frame that draws a visual from that layer, and only the quads whose hashes differ from what it holds (`lbterm_motion::content::dirty_runs`, runs closer than 32 quads as one), in order on the queue, so frames already submitted draw what they were given. A spinner's row written again writes about 10 KB; a pane scrolling writes its whole screen, 3.7 MB at 1,920 pixels, since every line moves. A quad is cleared as it's allocated, as a buffer made for the frame was, so what it isn't given is the same every frame. A layer that needs more room is made half as big again at least (`content::grow`), so a pane filling up makes it again a few times rather than every 128 quads, each time with the pass painted twice; it keeps that room, its own copy and the GPU's, where it kept three of each before.
- *Bind groups made again.* The glyph atlas's view and its two bind groups, and a uniform buffer and its bind group for each visual drawn, were made every frame. They're kept now (termwindow/gpu_work.rs): the atlas's while it's the same atlas (its `Rc` held, so another can't take its address), each uniform while its projection and the text's colour transform are the same, let go after 600 frames unused, or once the window has been drawn at two other sizes since it was last drawn with (a projection is made from the window's size, so one made at a size the window has left can't be drawn with till it's back: `lbterm_motion::content::UniformsAt`, keeping the last size's so a restore after a maximize makes none again), and no more than `config::lbterm::GPU_UNIFORMS_KEPT` of them (256), past which the half drawn longest ago go. Each holds a buffer, which D3D12 places on 64 KB of the GPU's memory however small, so they're bounded by that, 16 MB, where a window resized 600 times kept 5,592 of them in 490 MB of GPU memory before (lbterm/PITFALLS.md, traps 111 and 112; lbterm/PERF.md, finding 96); and with D3D12 each takes a view in the device's view heap (`GPU_VIEW_DESCRIPTORS`, 65,536). The shader reads no clock, so `milliseconds` is 0 in every one.

Surfaces are pooled, not owned by panes: a pane keeps its surface while it stays visible (for continuity during motion), and a tab switch reassigns free surfaces, resizing them if needed; the tab left keeps its own while they fade (`tab_switch`), and they go back to the pool once that's done. ## Motion

Written as data in `lbterm.lua`, never as per-frame Lua (Lua runs on the GUI thread):

```lua
config.lbterm_motion = {
  enabled = 'on',                         -- 'on' | 'off' | 'system' (follow Windows' Animation effects)
  slowdown = 1.0,                         -- stretches every animation, for judging by eye
  pane_layout = 'spring(220ms, 0)',       -- split, close, swap, rotate, zoom: one shared spring
  pane_enter  = 'spring(160ms, 0)',
  pane_exit   = '120ms cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)',
}
```

- Curves use CSS syntax: `cubic-bezier()`, `linear()`, `ease`, `ease-out`, `ease-in-out`, `linear`. Springs use Apple's and Motion's `spring(duration, bounce)`: with mass 1, stiffness = (2π/duration)² and damping ratio = 1 − bounce. `spring(stiffness=800, damping=1.0)` is accepted too (niri's and Material's form).
- A retarget mid-flight starts from the visual's current value and carries its velocity. LBTerm knows both, because it computed the curve.
- `enabled = 'system'` follows Windows' Animation effects (`SPI_GETCLIENTAREAANIMATION`). It's the default. Lachlan's config sets `'on'`, because that switch is off on his PC.

What moves:

- Panes that only change position (swap, rotate) animate their offset.
- Panes that change size (a split shrinking a pane, a close growing its neighbours) resize their swapchain at once, so the PTY gets one resize, never one per frame. The visual's clip animates from the old size to the new while the content stays anchored at its top-left.
- A new pane rides in beside the moving split: its offset animates from the edge it was split from, inside a container visual clipped to the old rectangle of the pane it came out of (so it never draws over a neighbour), and it fades in on `pane_enter`.
- A closing pane's visual stays alive with its last frame, moves to the bottom of the pane group so its growing neighbours cover it, fades out on `pane_exit`, then returns to the pool. A pane that comes back before the fade ends is picked up where it is.
- A tab switched from, or one that closes, fades into the next one: its panes' visuals stay alive with their last frame (nothing presents to them again), move to the top of the pane group, over the next tab's panes (there at once, drawn under them, the keyboard already in them), and fade out on `tab_switch` (`Change::Dissolve`, from `lbterm_motion::dissolve`, only when every pane that left isn't in the tab shown now: an overlay opening over a tab's panes leaves them in it, and it comes at once). The strip's words dissolve into the next tab's with them, on `swap`, the same curve. So do the strips down the right, the scroll strip's thumb and marks and the objectives strip's marks (render/sides.rs): the strips are drawn in `over` from a list of shapes kept each frame, and in a frame whose panes all gave way to others (`lbterm_motion::gave_way`) the last frame's list is drawn again into a visual of its own, `sides`, which `prepare_sides` puts over them and fades on `tab_switch` from the share the panes start from, once `prepare` says the panes dissolved; strips that look the same in both tabs aren't drawn again. A pane that comes back with nothing to carry it in shows whole at once: a tab switched back to while it still fades is taken out of the fade, whole, with what it shows now (its panes are painted again that frame), under the tab it was fading over, which starts its own fade from as much of it as showed (`lbterm_motion::dissolve_from`), so the screen goes on from what it was. ## Checking it without a window

Since feat/dcomp-recorder (Lachlan's 3c of 2026-11-09, beside his 3a and 3b, which landed as feat/look-tests). Until then the only way to see what compositor.rs builds was a test window: 52 of the 116 scripts in `lbterm/perf` judge pixels or count frames in one, and they fail for nothing whenever another program's window comes up, the PC is locked, the displays sleep or a window covers the test's (of 529 failed e2e attempts kept from 30 October to 9 November, 6 were a branch's own: `bun .claude/tools/e2e-history.ts`). The maths of motion is pure and tested already (`lbterm-motion`: curves, springs, the planner, `lbterm_motion::menu`'s fade, light, bump and flash). What only a window showed is what compositor.rs hands DirectComposition: which visual, in what order in the tree, which property animated from what to what, when a lamp or an effect goes on and comes off. So the tests build the compositor over a stand-in device that records every call, drive its `prepare_*` functions, and check the tree and the animations it built, in cargo, in the check, in milliseconds. This section is the design (stage 1 of the track) and what's built of it: the stand-in (stage 2), `with_device` and the first tests of the compositor over it (stage 3).

### The stand-in

`wezterm-gui/src/termwindow/compositor/recorder.rs`, declared at the end of compositor.rs under `#[cfg(all(test, windows))]`, so none of it is in the exe; its own tests are `compositor/recorder/tests.rs`. Each stand-in is a COM object made with the windows crate's `#[implement]`, so compositor.rs calls it through the same interfaces, vtables and `cast()`s as it calls DirectComposition's own, and needs no change to be called so. They share one `Recorder`, an `Arc<Mutex<Log>>` (a stand-in holds a clone; the mutex because `#[implement]` makes agile objects, and a stand-in may be released on whichever thread drops it). Each is known in the log by a kind, the word `Call::Made` and the tree's text give it:

- **The device** (`device`): one object implementing `IDCompositionDesktopDevice` and `IDCompositionDevice3` (both inherit `IDCompositionDevice2`, implemented once), so `cast()` from one to the other works as it does on the real one. It makes the other stand-ins: `CreateVisual`, `CreateRectangleClip`, `CreateAnimation`, `CreateRotateTransform`, `CreateScaleTransform`, `CreateTargetForHwnd` (the window's handle and `topmost` are taken and never used), `CreateColorMatrixEffect`, `CreateHueRotationEffect`; and `Commit`.
- **The target** (`target`): `SetRoot`, recorded as the target's `Root` link. A visual with a parent, or another target's root, fails with `E_INVALIDARG`.
- **The visual** (`visual`): `IDCompositionVisual3`, and so `IDCompositionVisualDebug`, `IDCompositionVisual2` and `IDCompositionVisual`, all one object: `OffsetX`, `OffsetY` and `Opacity` as values or animations, `BitmapInterpolationMode` as a value (the mode's number), `AddVisual`, `RemoveVisual`, and its links `Content` (only something from outside the recorder: a swapchain), `Clip` (a rectangle clip of ours), `Effect` (a colour matrix, a hue rotation or a transform of ours: in DirectComposition a transform is an effect too) and `Transform` (a rotate or scale transform of ours), each given an object or none.
- **The animation** (`animation`): `AddCubic` (begin, then the cubic's four coefficients, kept as lbterm_motion's own `Cubic`), `AddRepeat` (begin, duration), `End` (time, value).
- **The rectangle clip** (`clip`): `Left`, `Top`, `Right` and `Bottom` and the eight corner radii (`TopLeftRadiusX` to `BottomRightRadiusY`), each a value or an animation.
- **The effects**: the colour matrix (`colour matrix`): `SetMatrixElement(row, column, ..)`, a value or an animation, named `Matrix[row][column]` (`recorder::matrix(row, column)` gives the name), a row or column outside the matrix failing with `E_INVALIDARG`; and `ClampOutput` (1 or 0). DirectComposition's colour matrix is 5×4: rows 0 to 3 say what each input channel (red, green, blue, alpha) gives each output channel (the columns, in the same order), and row 4 is what's added to each, so `Matrix[4][0]` is red's offset. The hue rotation (`hue rotation`): `Angle`.
- **The transforms**: rotation (`rotate`: `Angle`, `CenterX`, `CenterY`) and scale (`scale`: `ScaleX`, `ScaleY`, `CenterX`, `CenterY`), each a value or an animation.

A property is named as its method is without `Set` and the value overload's `2`. Both overloads of every property above are recorded, the animation's included where compositor.rs only sets a value today (the transforms' centres): a recorded property is recorded whichever way it's given. Every other method of these interfaces is one compositor.rs doesn't call (`SetBorderMode`, `RemoveAllVisuals`, `SetVisible`, `SetOffsetZ`, `AddSinusoidal`, `Reset`, the colour matrix's `SetMatrix` and `SetAlphaMode`, every effect's `SetInput`, `CreateSurface`, `CreateTranslateTransform`, `WaitForCommitCompletion` and the rest). It's recorded as `Unrecorded` and fails with `E_NOTIMPL`, so compositor.rs's `?` makes it the test's failure: nothing answers "done" without being recorded, and code that starts calling a new method fails its test until the stand-in records it. Nothing a test can do makes a stand-in panic: a panic can't unwind out of an `extern "system"` function, so it aborts the whole test binary, every other test's result with it. For the same reason the log's lock is taken back from a test that panicked holding it.

Each object is known by its COM identity, the pointer its `IUnknown` has whichever interface asks. It's either one of the recorder's (`Object::Ours(id)`) or something from outside it (`Object::Foreign(id)`): the swapchains wgpu makes, numbered the first time a call is given one, and held while the recorder lives, as a visual holds its content, so a freed swapchain's address can't come back as another's under the same number. Ours and foreign share one count from 1, in the order objects are made or first seen, so a test that makes the same things in the same order gets the same numbers. A stand-in takes its address out of the log as it's released (`Me`'s `Drop`), so something made later at the same address isn't taken for it; the tree keeps its number. Where one of the recorder's own is wanted, an object it didn't make (another device's visual or animation) fails with `E_INVALIDARG`, as DirectComposition fails another device's objects, and so does one of its own of the wrong kind; where something from outside is wanted (content), one of its own fails the same way.

What DirectComposition does that a test could pass wrongly without, the stand-in does too:

- `AddVisual(child, above, reference)`: with no reference, `above` FALSE puts the child on top of its siblings and TRUE at the bottom; with one, just above or below it. A child that has a parent already or is a target's root, a reference that isn't a child, and a visual added under itself or one of its own children fail with `E_INVALIDARG`, as `RemoveVisual` does for a visual that isn't a child. A refused call leaves nothing in the log.
- A property given a value lets go of its animation, and one given an animation lets go of its value.
- An animation is copied when it's handed to a property (DirectComposition's documentation of `SetOffsetX(IDCompositionAnimation*)`: a change to the animation after doesn't reach the property until it's set again). compositor.rs finishes every one before it does (`drive`, `play`, `lap` and the lamps), so what the copy holds is what DWM plays. A segment or an end added to an animation after it's been handed over is recorded with `late: true`, for a test to fail on.
- The tree, values, animations and links take effect at `Commit`: the recorder keeps what's pending (`live`) apart from what was committed (`tree`). It records the commit's moment (`Call::Commit { at }`), and each animation handed to a property since the last commit starts playing at it, as DWM starts it, whenever the compositor worked it out: `Tree::started(object, property)` gives that moment, kept until the property is given a value or another animation, and `Tree::shows(object, property, t)` what the property shows at `t`, its animation played from it (PITFALLS 304).

Where DirectComposition's documentation says nothing, the stand-in has rules of its own, each refusing what would be a bug on the screen if it were let through: a value that isn't a number (NaN, infinite) fails with `E_INVALIDARG`; so do a segment that begins before the one before it or below 0, a repeat with nothing before it to repeat or with no length, an `End` before the last segment's beginning, a second `End`, and a segment after the `End`.

The log is every call in order (`Call`): an object made (`Made`), a value set (`Set`), an animation handed to a property (`Animated`, with its copy), a child added or removed (`Add`, `Remove`), a link (`Linked`: content, clip, effect, transform, a target's root), a segment or an end added to an animation (`Segment`, `End`, with `late`), a commit (`Commit { at }`, with its moment), and a call it doesn't record (`Unrecorded`). What a test reads, the reader API:

- `Recorder::device()`: the stand-in device as an `IDCompositionDesktopDevice`, for `Compositor::with_device` (below).
- `tree()`: a `Tree`, everything as it stood at the last commit. `live()`: the same as the calls have left it, what isn't committed yet included. A `Tree` reads: `root()` (the first target's; `roots()` gives each target with its root), `children(o)` (bottom to top), `parent(o)`, `walk()` (every visual under a root, each after its parent and its children bottom to top, with its depth), `shown(o)` (whether it's under a root, so on the screen), `kind(o)`, `prop(o, "Opacity")` (a `Prop`: `Value(f32)` or `Animation(Curve)`, and `Prop::at(t)` what it shows at a time either way), `value(o, ..)` and `curve(o, ..)` for one or the other, `props(o)` (every property by name), and `link(o, "Clip")`. Its `Display` is the tree as text, for a failure's message: each target, each visual indented by its depth with its properties (`visual 4 (OffsetX 10, Opacity 0 to 1.3 in 200 ms (0 to 1.32))`: an animation as from, to and how long, with how far it goes past its ends in brackets, a loop as `every` its lap), and under each what it links, with their properties (`Clip: clip 5 (Right 20)`, `Content: foreign 7`).
- `pending()`: the calls since the last commit that change what's shown (`Set`, `Animated`, `Add`, `Remove`, `Linked`). It's empty once a frame is done; if not, what the frame changed shows late, at whatever commits next.
- `mark()`, then `since(mark)`: every call after it, and `animated(mark)`: each animation handed to a property after it, as a `Handed` (`id`, `property`, `curve`). `calls()` is the whole log.
- A `Curve`, the copy (`animation`, its `segments`, each a `Segment::Cubic(lbterm_motion::Cubic)` or a `Segment::Repeat { begin, duration }`, and its `end`), reads: `value(t)`, what DWM shows `t` seconds after the commit that starts it (the cubic `t` is in, a repeat's lap again, the end's value from the end on, and before the first segment where that starts), which for what `drive` and `play` give is `lbterm_motion::Segments::value`'s, as the tests hold; `ends()`; `lap()` (`Some` for a loop); `segments()`, its cubics as lbterm_motion's `Segments` up to its end or first repeat; and `played()`, a `lbterm_extras::state::Played`.
- `object(&interface)`: what the recorder knows one of the compositor's own objects as (`compositor.menus`, a pane's container, a layer's swapchain), so a test inside `mod imp` finds it in the tree; `foreign(o)`: a foreign object the recorder holds, to cast to what it is (`IDXGISwapChain1`).

The names of what plays are the compositor's own bookkeeping, not the recorder's: `Compositor::animations(now)` gives each name in flight with what it `played` (`[{from, to, low, high, ms}]`, lbterm/STATE.md), from the same `Segments` DWM is given. `Curve::played()` is that bookkeeping again, not a second one: it rebuilds the `Segments` from what DWM was given and calls the same `Segments::span`, so a test can check that the state file's numbers under a name and what a visual's property was handed are the same numbers. They are exactly for `play`'s animations. For `drive`'s with a delay, `ms` counts the delay, which the state file's starts after, and `low` and `high` are read on a grid moved by it. For a loop `played()` gives one lap: from where it starts to where its first repeat begins, `ms` the lap. A curve that neither ends nor repeats (its last cubic played for ever, which compositor.rs never hands over) has none.

### Surfaces

A pane's or a layer's surface is wgpu's, made over a visual (`instance.create_surface_unsafe(wgpu::SurfaceTargetUnsafe::CompositionVisual(visual.as_raw()))`, in `Layer::new` and `create_under_surface`). wgpu-hal 25.0.2's D3D12 surface keeps the visual (an `AddRef`), and as it's configured makes a composition swapchain (`IDXGIFactory2::CreateSwapChainForComposition`) and gives it to the visual with `IDCompositionVisual::SetContent`: the one call wgpu makes on it (`dx12/mod.rs`, `Surface::configure`, `SurfaceTarget::Visual`). It needs no window, and compositor.rs reads the swapchain back through `as_hal::<Dx12>` (`swap_chain`) for the visuals that show it too.

So the tests run the compositor over a real D3D12 device on WARP, Windows' software adapter (`force_fallback_adapter`, in every Windows), and every surface it makes is real: no seam, and production's code unchanged. The spike's second test holds it: a wgpu surface over a stand-in visual, configured on WARP, gives the visual one foreign object through `SetContent`, and it's an `IDXGISwapChain1`. WARP runs on the CPU, so the tests hold no GPU: they need no `--gpu-draw`, and don't wait on or share the GPU with Earshot or a game. The device is one for the test binary, made the first time a test asks and never dropped (`recorder::gpu()`, which the compositor's tests share), and a test takes its turn with it: `gpu()` gives a `Turn` that holds a static `Mutex` while it lives, so no two tests' threads configure or drop surfaces on it at once, which wgpu-hal's D3D12 device can't take (one auto-reset event for every such wait, PITFALLS 303). It's one device and never dropped because wgpu-hal loads `dxgi.dll` and `d3d12.dll` itself and frees them with its instance and device, and the swapchains the recorder holds released after that call into a library that's gone (PITFALLS, 293).

Weighed and not taken: a seam keeping surfaces out of the tests (a trait that makes a layer's surface, or a `Layer` without one). `Layer` holds its `wgpu::Surface` and configuration everywhere in compositor.rs, `acquire_*` draw into them, and `prepare` takes the `wgpu::Instance`, `Device` and `SurfaceConfiguration`, so the seam would touch most of compositor.rs's 123 public functions and every merge of the branches that change it; production would have to go through it too (no second path), and it buys nothing WARP doesn't. wgpu's no-op backend makes no surface over a composition visual.

The tests prepare and commit; they needn't acquire, draw or present, which is what the perf scripts' captures and `lbterm.ps1 look` show. They can, on WARP, if one needs to (the `queue` is kept for it).

### The one change to production: `Compositor::with_device`

`Compositor::new(hwnd)` makes the device with `DCompositionCreateDevice3` and calls `Compositor::with_device(device, hwnd, numbers)` (private to `mod imp`), which holds the rest of what `new` did, line for line, in the same `unsafe` block: the target, the root, the twenty visuals in their order, the card templates' clip, the commit. Production goes through it as the tests do, one path; the only lines `new` has of its own are the device's. A test calls `with_device(recorder.device(), null, true)`.

`numbers` is the field `record` asks before working out what an animation played (`played`, lbterm/STATE.md). `new` passes `lbterm_extras::state::path().is_some()`, the `OnceLock` read once from `LBTERM_STATE_FILE` that `record` read itself before, so a window with a state file works them out and Lachlan's doesn't, as before; a test passes `true`. A test sets no environment variable: the process's environment is every test's at once.

Built in stage 3, with the first tests of the compositor over the stand-in (below, "The compositor's tests").

### The compositor's tests

`termwindow/compositor/imp/tests.rs`, inside `mod imp`, so they read the compositor's own fields (its visuals, `layers`' panes, the outline) and find each in the tree through `Recorder::object`. Since stage 3 of feat/dcomp-recorder.

- **The fixture.** `Screen` is a `Recorder`, a `Compositor` made over its device with `with_device(recorder.device(), null, true)`, and the test's turn with the WARP device (`recorder::gpu()`'s `Turn`, in a field declared after the compositor's, so it's let go after every surface is dropped; the compositor's tests take turns with the device, PITFALLS 303, and a test that makes a second screen drops the first). `Screen::frame(panes, active, motion, left)` is a paint's part of it: `prepare` with WARP's instance and device (`recorder::gpu()`), a base configuration as webgpu.rs makes it with the compositor on (`Bgra8Unorm`, `Fifo`, opaque, `COPY_SRC`), an 800×600 window, card templates as wide and as tall as it (32 pixels), no tab panel, nothing in `top`, a dark backdrop; then `commit_if_changed`, as render/composited.rs commits after presenting; then it fails if anything is left `pending()`, or if since the last frame's end a segment or `End` was added to an animation already handed to a property (`late`, which DWM wouldn't play, PITFALLS 294) or a method the stand-in doesn't record was called (`Unrecorded`), so every frame of every test judges both (`Screen::commit`, since the fix-up). `pane(id)` gives a pane's container, visual, clip and bounds clip (its `PaneLayer`, on screen or fading out), `outline()` the outline's group, `panes()` the visual the panes' containers are in, `played(name, since)` the state file's numbers under a name. `settings(on)` is the config's motion settings as they come (`LbtermMotion::default()`, `enabled` on or off, through `MotionSettings::from_config`), and `on(curve, from, to, epsilon)` what `drive` hands DWM for a curve from still with no delay (`Motion::segments(STEP, epsilon)`), which a test compares with `Curve::segments()` exactly: the cubics DWM was given are those numbers.
- **What they hold.** The window's visuals: the twenty children of the root in their order (the table in "How a frame reaches the screen"), the card templates' clip with no size, all committed. A split: the pane split stays where it was, its clip's `Right` narrowing to its half on `pane_layout`; the new pane's container on top of the panes, clipped to the rectangle it came out of (its `bounds`), its visual riding in from that rectangle's right edge on `pane_layout` and fading in on `pane_enter`; the outline gliding onto it on `focus_move`; the state file's `pane.enter` numbers the same as the fade's, and `pane.layout` and `outline.move` in flight. A close: the closed pane's container moved under the one taking its room, fading out where it was on `pane_exit`, the one left widening on `pane_layout`, the outline gliding back; once `fading_panes` says the fade is done, the next frame takes the closed pane out of the tree. A tab switch (`left` true for the tab left's panes): the next tab's pane there whole under the tab left's two, which stay where they were and fade from all of them to nothing by the time `tab_switch` ends, their numbers the state file's `pane.dissolve`. The keyboard moving between two panes: the outline glides on `focus_move` and nothing is handed to a pane's container, visual or clip, nor anything named `pane.layout`. Motion off: the same split with nothing handed to DWM and nothing in flight, every value where it ends.
- **The menus and the rail** (stage 4, `imp/tests/menus.rs` and `imp/tests/rail.rs`, child modules of `imp/tests.rs` that share its `Screen`). `Screen::menus(tab, motion)` is a paint's `prepare_menus` with the tab menu's slot as `tab` and the others none, `Screen::lights(lights, motion)` its `prepare_lights` (the window's lights, render/lights.rs), each ended by `Screen::commit()` as `frame` is; `card()`, `ghost()` and `lamp()` find the slot's group, card, highlight, other page and the lamp's two visuals, `menus_visual()` and `menu_lights()` the two visuals the cards and their flashes are in. A `MenuVisual` is made as render/menu_fade.rs makes one (a rect, open or fading, its hairline as a `MenuRing`, the keys with nowhere to go and the page turns as counts). They hold: a card's group in `menus`, the card and its highlight in it, fading in on `pane_enter` from nothing and out on `pane_exit` under `menu.tab`, nothing more handed while it's open at rest, put away unseen once the slot has none, and with motion off there and gone at once with no lamp; a key with nowhere to go (a count up by one, the card not just opened) making the lamp, its light (`halo`) just under the card and its hairline's group just over it and under the highlight, both on one chain (`lbterm_motion::menu::bump`, up to `MENU_BUMP_HAIRLINE` on `arrive_in` and back to nothing on `panel_glow`) under `menu.tab.hairline`, out of the group by the first frame after its end and back for the next key, the same visuals; a page turn swapping the card's two surfaces, the old page's over the new page's and under the highlight, fading on `swap` under `menu.tab.swap`, the new page at 1 at once, the next turn swapping them back with nothing made, and with motion off no turn; and a picked row's flash (`Light::Row`) in `menu_lights`, never the card's group, at the row, on `lbterm_motion::menu::flash` under `menu.tab.flash`, ending after the card's fade out, still in the tree once the card is put away and out of it by the first frame of lights after its end. `rail.rs`'s `panel(rows, open, motion, spin)` is a closed rail's `PanelPlan` as render/tab_panel.rs makes one, from an `lbterm_panel::Layout` for the 800×600 window (each row a tab's key and a look, the marker on `open`'s slot, no progress, drag, rings, spinners or wave), and `Screen::rail(plan, motion)` a frame of one pane with it. They hold: the open tab closed, the row below sliding up into its slot with the marker riding on it, `rail.marker`'s `left_ms` within 5 ms of the slowest `rail.row.slide`'s and its visual's `OffsetY` going from where the row showed to the rail's top; under a menu's card (`PanelPlan::cards`) a close, an open, a new look and the marker moving hand DWM nothing and name nothing `rail.` (the rows' and the marker's names: the plan has no progress, so the progress fills the compositor holds too are left to the windows' `New-UnderCardWatch`), and the frame after the card has gone plays the exit, the slide and the marker; and the loops, a waiting pad's arc (`spinners`), the Conductor's outline's hues (`rings`, `RingStyle::Spectrum`) and its light, each property that repeats found by walking the tree and what each visual links: four with `spin` on (the arc's angle, the hues' angle, the light's x and y) and `Compositor::loops()` all three names, none with it off and `loops()` empty, the arc at angle 0, the outline with no effect and the light at its first place, all still shown, and the four back with it on.
- **Tabs in flight, a connection's light and the Conductor's spectrum** (stage 5, `imp/tests/flight.rs`, `imp/tests/connections.rs` and the last test of `imp/tests/rail.rs`). `Screen::frame` is `paint` (a paint's `prepare`) and `commit`, so a frame can prepare more before its commit. `flight.rs`'s `Screen::tab(tab, motion)` is a frame of one of five tabs (tab one split in two with the keyboard in its right pane, the rest a pane each, `left` true for every pane not the tab's) with `prepare_sides` after `prepare`, as render/composited.rs calls them, its picture over a strip 40 pixels wide down the window's right; motion is the config's at `slowdown` 5, so `tab_switch` is a second and four tabs are in flight however long WARP takes to make a tab's surface. After each frame a `Shot` keeps the frame's commit (the recorder's `Call::Commit { at }`), the committed tree, the tab on screen and every opacity in the two stacks, each played from the commit that handed it over (`Tree::started`), as DWM plays it (PITFALLS 304), with its lag: that commit less the moment the compositor worked it out at (a pane's `PaneLayer::alpha` start, `prepare`'s `now`; a strip picture's `done` less its curve's length, `prepare_sides`' `now`). From those it works out each tab's share as DWM composes the stacks, top to bottom, each visual showing its opacity of what the ones over it leave: the panes' at a point in each half of the window (`panes_share`), the strips' over the tab on screen's own (`sides_share`). It switches from tab one to five 40 ms apart and back to one 40 ms after (Lachlan's 3a: four in flight, the oldest switched back to), and holds: at each switch nothing on screen, panes or strips, moves further than the two frames' flights do in the spread of their lags (each tab's share as the frame before plays it at this one's commit against this one's, to what each flight moves over the spread, the two together, since they part at the switch, and 0.01); read every 2 ms from each commit to the next and on until the last tab in flight is done, no tab in flight shows more of itself, in its panes or on the strips, than 2 ms before (to 0.005), tab one's two panes show the same share, the strips each tab's share as its panes do but for what the flight moves in the spread of the frame's lags (to that and 0.01: PITFALLS 298's lead of the strips, which DWM shows), the spread under 100 ms, and tab one, switched back to from somewhere between, only rises, to all of it; and once they're done the next frame puts every pane and picture in flight away. With `--nocapture` it says the lags it saw; on 26 runs of the fix-up's (2026-11-10, WARP on 4 CPUs) the lags of one frame's curves were at most 4 to 10 ms apart, and 2 to 11 ms across a switch. Until the fix-up it played each curve from the moment it was worked out at, which held the compositor's own idea of the timing and none of what DWM shows (PITFALLS 304), with the strips held to the panes to 0.01. `connections.rs`'s `Screen::lamps(plan, play)` is a paint's `prepare_connection_lamps` with a `GlowPlan` of one upright line, its light's path from lbterm_extras::links::glow_path and a lap of 0.2 s so the three laps are over in a test's time: the light's visual in `panel_lights` at the line's x, its `OffsetY` three laps ending at the line's middle (at each lap's start the middle, a quarter on the bottom, three quarters on the top, never off the line), `connection.light` in `loops()`; after them the next paint sets it still at the middle by value, the same visual, nothing animated and the loop gone; a new `says` runs them again on a new visual, no plan takes it out, and with `play` false (motion off, the window unseen) it's still at the middle from the start. `rail.rs`'s last test gives the Conductor's own pad a ring as render/tab_panel.rs does (`RingStyle::Spectrum`, no light, a `RingFade`): none at rest and nothing turning; lit, it fades in from nothing on `panel_glow` exactly (`on`) under `conductor.outline.fade`, the state file's numbers the same as the curve's, its `Effect` a hue rotation whose `Angle` goes once round every `HUE_PERIOD` (0 at 0, 180 at half, 90 a quarter into the second lap) and `loops()` names `conductor.outline`; let go once it's whole, the same ring fades out from all of it on the same curve under the same name; dropped from the plan, it's out of the tree, nothing turning; and in read only (`Curve::Instant`) its opacity is 0 at once with nothing handed to DWM and no fade named.
- **Traps for a new test**: the first frame of a `Compositor` is a resize, so nothing animates in it (PITFALLS 295); `animations(now)` forgets what has ended by `now`, so the played numbers are asked at a time from before the frame (PITFALLS 296); and a wait for an animation to end is timed from after the frame that started it, never from before it: the frame that makes a surface on WARP can take longer than the animation (PITFALLS 297). `prepare` and `prepare_sides` each take their own moment, so curves from one frame are worked out apart, and DWM starts them all at the frame's commit (PITFALLS 298, 304): a model of the screen plays each from its commit and allows for the lags it measures, no more. The tests take turns with the WARP device (PITFALLS 303): a test that makes a second `Screen` drops the first, or it waits on its own turn for ever. A window check comes out of its script only when the test holds the whole rule, the plan render makes of the real input included (PITFALLS 305). Two things stage 4 found in the code that a test meets: `prepare_lights` takes the lights that are done out before it returns early for no lights or motion off, so a frame with no lights still puts a done flash away; and the rail marker's visual is offset from the rail surface's top (`drive_off`'s `y0`), not from its slot's, so its `OffsetY` is read against the surface. And wezterm-gui is on the 2018 edition, where an `assert!` with one string doesn't format it (PITFALLS 144): give a failure's names as arguments; the test modules `deny(non_fmt_panics)`, so one is a compile error.
- **What they cost.** The six tests made wezterm-gui's run go from 49 tests in 5.0 s to 55 in 5.2 s (they run beside the others; each makes its own surfaces on WARP, a pane's at its size, the four templates and `over`), and the test binary compiles in about 30 s after a change to compositor.rs alone. Stage 4's eight (five of the menus', three of the rail's) made it 63 in 5.25 s, the run beside them; the menus' sleep through each animation they wait out (up to about a second for the flash), which the others' run covers. Stage 5's four (the tabs in flight, two of the connection's light, the Conductor's pad) made it 67 in 5.6 s: the flight's runs about 1.3 s (a second of `tab_switch` at `slowdown` 5 after its switches) and reads some 600 moments, the light's sleeps out three laps of 0.2 s, and the pad's out each fade on `panel_glow`. Two runs hung, blocked with no CPU: stage 5's (the flight's test failing, the pad's then blocked for five minutes until its binary was ended) and one of eight of the review's reruns of the compositor's tests (the pad's test again, the flight's passed). Two tests' threads configured or dropped surfaces on the one WARP device at once, both waiting on wgpu-hal's one auto-reset event (PITFALLS 303); since the fix-up they take turns with the device (`Turn`), so the compositor's tests run one after another, and wezterm-gui's run went from 67 tests in 5.6 s to 67 in 6.9 s. The fix-up's reruns, through warden-run on 4 CPUs on 2026-11-10: wezterm-gui's run once (67 passed in 6.85 s), then its built test binary's 28 compositor tests 25 times (`compositor --nocapture`, each round ended had it passed 120 s): none hung or failed, each round 6.95 to 7.21 s, where they took 2.5 to 2.9 s side by side in the review's reruns. A hung test holds the check until the warden's `--max`, so a run whose last line says a test `has been running for over 60 seconds` is still one to end and report.
- **What stays with the windows.** The perf scripts that judge these motions in a real window judge more than the compositor: `tab-switch.ps1`'s first frame on tab two with `pane.dissolve` in flight and none with motion off, `state.ps1`'s split seen in the file and `present.ps1`'s motions go through the mux switching the tab, render/composited.rs's `left` (which panes left with their tab, from the mux's active tab and whether the Conductor's view covers them), `state.rs` writing the file and DWM's own playback, where a test here gives `prepare` its panes and `left` and stops at the tree. So none of their checks moved here in stage 3, and `.claude/docs/perf-scripts.md` is as it was. A later check that a script makes of the compositor alone (what it builds from given panes) is one to move. Stage 4 moved two: menu-keys.ps1's step 16 judged the flare's played numbers (up to `MENU_BUMP_HAIRLINE` and back to 0, and done once the window settled), the compositor's alone given the plan, so the script keeps its captures, the name that says a key reached the compositor (`menu.tab.hairline`) and the menu left open; and tab-mouse.ps1 the marker's `left_ms` within 5 ms of the slowest slide's at each of the three closes of the open tab, which the fix-up put back in the script: whether the marker rides or glides turns on the plan render/tab_panel.rs makes of each real close (the closed tab's row gone from `rows` as the marker moves), which rail.rs's hand-built plan doesn't judge, and the check left in its place (`rail.marker` beside `rail.row.slide`) passed a glide too (PITFALLS 305). The script judges the real closes' plans, rail.rs the compositor's half. What stays with the windows for the menus and the rail, and why: a key or a click reaching a menu (keyevent.rs, render/tab_menu.rs, render/menu_fade.rs, which give `MenuVisual`'s counts: a flash's, a swap's or the hairline's name in the state file is what says it got there); the panel's hold before the plan (render/tab_panel.rs's `Hover::hold` for the edge, light, words and sweep, the glint and send lights): `New-UnderCardWatch` judges every Held name of `Get-RailUnderCard` under the cards, and this test only those the compositor holds (the rows', the marker's, the progress fills'); the wave stopping under a card (menu-keys.ps1's step 10, `WavePlan`, not tested here yet); the menus' light's falloff (step 15, pixels of the card's own surface); and dwm-loops.ps1's minimize to `window_unseen` to `loops_play` to `spin`, its paints while minimized and DWM turning what it was handed. Stage 5 moved: tab-switch.ps1's step 9 judging each tab's share of its panes in flight (never rising, not stepping, tab one back from what it showed and rising) and side-strips.ps1's step 3b (each tab's scroll strip keeping its panes' share, two's never rising again, one's never jumping at the third press), both the compositor's given the panes each switch left and the strips' picture, which flight.rs holds as DWM plays it since the fix-up, each curve from its commit, with the lags it measures allowed for and no more (until then it played each from the moment it was worked out at, the compositor's own idea of the timing, and judged nothing of the screen's: PITFALLS 304); tab-connections.ps1's light still at the line's middle after its laps and with motion off; and conductor.ps1's `conductor.outline` among the loops while the pad acts.
