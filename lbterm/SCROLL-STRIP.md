# Scroll strip

A narrow strip down the right of the window, just left of the objectives panel and looking like it, that's the open tab's scrollbar. In Claude Code's fullscreen view it's the conversation's: a thumb where Claude Code is scrolled to, and a mark where each of your prompts is and where Claude did something worth finding again. At a shell it's the scrollback's, with a mark at each prompt. A click on a mark goes there, a click on the track goes to that point, the thumb drags, and resting the pointer on a mark opens a card saying what it is.

It's Lachlan's ask (2026-10-28): he didn't know what the right bar (the objectives strip) was telling him, and wanted the scrollbar to reflect the open conversation, with marks at his prompts that take him there and marks for other Claude events. He kept the objectives strip with its projects pad, and picked (his answers 2a, 3abcdefghij, 4a, 5 yes, 6 yes):

- the strip is the active pane's only scrollbar: the panes' own goes, and its column with it;
- marks for all ten kinds of event below;
- the bar's length measured by the rows each message takes at the pane's width;
- a card on a mark with its words and time;
- dragging the thumb and clicking the track scroll Claude Code there.

Keys to hop to the previous or next prompt are parked for later (his 7).

## Turning it on

```lua
config.lbterm_scroll_strip = {
  width = '10.5pt',       -- the strip, its ring included (14 px at 96 DPI)
  card_width = '270pt',   -- the card on a mark (360 px at 96 DPI), less when the window hasn't the room
  open_delay_ms = 150,    -- the pointer rests on a mark this long before its card opens
  close_delay_ms = 200,   -- and it closes this long after the pointer leaves the mark and the card
  transcripts = nil,      -- Claude Code's transcripts, a folder per project; ~/.claude/projects when unset
}
```

It needs `lbterm_tab_panel`, whose rows it lines up with. Permission prompts need `lbterm_summaries` too (below). With it on, the panes' own scrollbar (`enable_scroll_bar`) goes, and the room `effective_right_padding` kept for it; a build without it keeps the scrollbar, so a config can set both.

## What it's the scrollbar of

The active pane's. When the pane names a Claude session (its `claude_session` user var, which Terminal's claudehook sets) and is on the alternate screen, it's Claude Code's fullscreen view, and the strip is the conversation's, standing beside the conversation's rows only: from the pane's top down to the row above Claude Code's input box, never beside the input box or the status under it. Anything else is the scrollback's, beside the whole pane: the thumb is the viewport, as the scrollbar's was, and a coral bar marks each OSC 133 prompt (`render/marks.rs`'s marks), a burst of them one bar. Each frame looks only at the rows that changed since its last, for a prompt read off each row's runs of attributes (`Line::has_semantic_type`, 0.25 us a row), with the pane's terminal held: working out each row's zones there took 4.5 us a row, and a pane printing at full speed spent 11.7 ms of every frame on it while its parser waited (lbterm/PERF.md, finding 69).

## A shell's marks

A shell marks the row each prompt starts on with OSC 133 (a zone of type Prompt; Lachlan's pwsh profile sends it), and the strip puts a coral bar where each is in the scrollback, a run of prompt rows (a prompt over two lines) one bar. A click on one scrolls the view so the prompt is at its top. The panes' own scrollbar, where a config keeps it, draws the same marks.

A scrollback runs to 50,000 rows with Lachlan's `scrollback_lines`, so nothing reads it whole more than once (`render/marks.rs`, `lbterm_extras::marks`, `mux/src/lbterm_prompts.rs`). Each look reads only what was written since the last (`marks::plan`): the rows that went into the scrollback since, which were on the screen then and may have changed before they went, and the screen's rows that changed. A row in the scrollback isn't written again, so it's read once. The first look at a pane, or one after a new width rewrapped its scrollback and numbered every row again, reads the screen at once and the scrollback a chunk of 5,000 rows at a time between frames, newest first, so a long one never holds a frame (about 7 ms a chunk of a build's log on this PC), with one frame after the last chunk for what they found. So does a look after more than a chunk of rows went into the scrollback since the last (a tab looked at, then printing a long log in the background, `marks::split_since`): until 2026-10-31 that look read every one of them in the frame that showed the tab, 80 ms of it locally after 50,000 rows and 17 ms on a mux domain. Each screen of a pane is followed apart: the alternate one numbers its rows from its own start.

A pane in the mux server (Lachlan's tabs) is a copy in the GUI that holds only the lines it has drawn. Its rows are read from the lines the GUI holds as the server has them, looked at where they are without fetching any (`wezterm-client/src/pane/lbterm_held.rs`), and the server is asked which rows of the rest hold a prompt: `GetPromptRows` (PDU 7503), answered with the prompt rows alone (7504), a chunk of 5,000 rows at a time, one ask under way. A screen row the GUI doesn't hold yet is read once it does (the GUI fetches what it draws). A server of an older build than the GUI (one installed while the daily server still runs the build before, until its last tab closes) answers that it doesn't know the PDU, naming it: the GUI says so once in its log (`scroll strip: the mux server doesn't know how to say which rows of pane ... hold prompts`) and marks only the prompts it has had on its screen until the server restarts. Any other error (a pane gone as it's respawned, a connection lost and made again) has its rows asked again at the first look a second on (`marks::server_lacks_prompt_rows`); until 2026-10-31 any error was taken for an old server.

Until 2026-10-31 each frame with output in the open tab went through every row of its scrollback, and on a mux pane the first look (each GUI start, a "Restart to update" included) asked the server for every line of it in one GetLines, made a blank line in the GUI for each until they came, and then marked none of them: what came back was no newer than the look, so it was never read again. What it cost, and costs now, is lbterm/PERF.md's "What a shell's marks cost".

Not while the Conductor's view is open (lbterm/TAB-PANEL.md, "The Conductor's view"): it covers the pane, the panes under it don't repaint the window, so the strip would say where a pane nobody can see is scrolled to, and a click would scroll it unseen. Leaving the view brings it back (since 2026-10-30; `perf/conductor-view.ps1` checks both).

## What Claude Code draws

Measured on Claude Code 2.1.312 in a test window on 2026-10-28, a throwaway Haiku session with a conversation of lists, a poem, a code block, a table, a long paragraph and tool calls (its transcript and every screen of it are lbterm-extras' `src/conversation/fixtures/claude-2.1.312`, the names that said whose PC it was swapped for others of the same length):

- At the top, a banner: a blank row, three rows of logo with the version, model and folder, two blank rows.
- Each message you sent: a blank row, then "❯ " and your words, white, on a grey band (`#373737`).
- Claude's words: "● " before the first line, two spaces before the rest, wrapped at spaces (Ink's wrap: a word that doesn't fit goes down whole, one longer than a row is broken), a list item's words hanging past its marker. Code fences aren't drawn, the code between them is; a table is boxed, with a rule above it, between every row and below it; a link shows its words without its address. Claude's thinking is drawn the same way where the transcript keeps its words.
- A run of tool calls: one line with no dot ("Read 1 file, ran 2 shell commands", "Made 1 scratchpad edit +4": an edit in the session's own scratchpad, `%TEMP%\claude\<project>\<session>\scratchpad`, folds too).
- An edit: "● Update(path)", "⎿ Added 2 lines, removed 1 line", then the diff (the transcript's `toolUseResult.structuredPatch`): each line numbered in a gutter as wide as the highest number, the code cut wherever the row ends, seven columns short of it; a line past 2000 characters cut there ("… [+3006 chars]"); "..." between hunks. A failed one: its header and one line.
- A new file: "● Write(path)", "⎿ Wrote 179 lines to path", then the file's first ten rows, numbered like a diff, and "… +172 lines". A file written over is drawn as an edit.
- A shell command that changed files (2.1.310's `bashEditDiff`): its command in two rows, three rows of its output, each file's diff (forty lines at most), and a two-row note. Other commands fold.
- A subagent, a skill: their header and one line. A background command or subagent finishing: "● Background command "…" completed (exit code 0)".
- A turn's end: "✻ Brewed for 2s · done 15:12".
- At the bottom: an effort line, the input box between two full-width rules ("─"), the status line's rows and the mode line. The conversation's area is the rows above the upper rule, less the effort line. A dialog in the input box's place (a plugin recommendation, a permission prompt) hides the rules, and the strip says it can't go anywhere until it's answered.
- PgUp and PgDn scroll half the area (21 rows of 43), Ctrl+Home to the top, Ctrl+End to the latest. A burst of them written at once is taken in one go and drawn once: on a 5,800-row conversation 40 PgDns took 260 ms, 120 took 530. Written one by one, Claude Code draws as it goes.
- While scrolled up, the area's first row is the sticky header, naming the message above the view, all of it on one line, cut with "…" when it doesn't fit: on the same band as a message, its words dim grey (`#505050`) where a message's are white. The area's last row says "Jump to bottom (ctrl+End) ↓" in its middle. At the latest neither shows. Once, after a burst of 120, Claude Code left "Jump to bottom" off a screen halfway up for seconds.
- Working, measured on 2.1.314 in a test window on 2026-10-31 against a stand-in for the API (`perf/claude-working.ps1`; the screens are lbterm-extras' `src/conversation/fixtures/claude-2.1.314-working`, the names that said whose PC it was swapped for others of the same length): at the latest the spinner is under the conversation, inside the area ("✻ Ruminating… ", its glyph turning through · ✢ * ✶ ✻ ✽ and its word shimmering, about six times a second), a tip under it ("⎿ Tip: …"), then the blank row over the input box's rule. Each tick changes only the spinner's row, but Claude Code writes its whole bottom block again for it. Scrolled up the spinner isn't drawn. A turn that ends while scrolled up says "1 new message (ctrl+End) ↓" where "Jump to bottom" was. In its place at the latest, the turn's end ("✻ Crunched for 0s · done 8:03").

The first version of the strip assumed a fixed six rows under the conversation, a header off the band, and a screen's rows matching the transcript row for row, and a stand-in written to those assumptions passed while the strip was wrong in the real one (2026-10-28). The second laid out the fixture's short Haiku session within a dozen rows, but one of Lachlan's real sessions at 1,127 rows where Claude Code drew 5,819: its edits' diffs, files and thinking weren't counted. The fixture is what its tests hold it to now, a real session of one's own can be checked the same way (Checking it), and `perf/fake-claude.ps1` draws the same way.

## The conversation

Claude Code's fullscreen view keeps the conversation to itself: the pane holds only the screen, and no escape sequence says where it's scrolled to. So the strip reads the session's transcript, `<transcripts>/<folder>/<session>.jsonl`, on a thread of its own as it grows (`lbterm_extras::conversation`, `crate::conversations`), into what Claude Code draws. Each window's strip says which session it shows (and its context pill's list, while it's open, says its own apart: `crate::conversations::Reader`, lbterm/CONTEXT.md), and the thread reads that one's transcript when it's first shown and whenever Windows says it changed, from where its last read stopped; a session no strip shows any more is kept while a pane names it, so showing it again reads only what was added. Only the windows showing a session repaint when it grows. What that costs is lbterm/AT-REST.md's "The scroll strip and the summaries".

- each prompt you sent, each run of Claude's words or thinking, each run of folded tool calls, each call shown on its own, each task's line, each turn's end, each interrupt and each compaction is a block, under the banner's rows;
- each block takes rows at the pane's width: its lines as Claude Code draws them (above), each line knowing how it wraps (`conversation::Line`: words, code cut at the row, a file's start capped at ten rows, a header always one row), and a line between blocks. On Lachlan's 5,800-row session that came to 5,814 rows against 5,819, each prompt within 3 rows of where Claude Code drew it;
- each line long enough to tell apart (six letters or digits) is indexed by its start, a diff's and a file's lines with their numbers as the row shows them, so any row of a message or a diff on screen says where it is;
- the marks are events on blocks.

| Mark | Kind | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| a prompt you sent (a slash command as itself) | `prompt` | a bar across, coral |
| a question Claude asked you: a picker, or a reply that ends asking | `question` | dot, orange |
| a permission prompt it showed you | `permission` | dot, amber |
| a tool call you turned down | `denied` | dot, red |
| a tool call that failed, or an API error | `error` | dot, red |
| a compaction | `compaction` | a line across; the part above it dimmed |
| a subagent, a background command or a Monitor started | `agent` | dot, teal |
| a commit, or a `land` | `commit` | dot, green |
| a file edited or written | `edit` | short tick, blue-grey |
| InMail sent, or work filed with `later` | `mail` | dot, violet |
| a turn's end | `turn-end` | short tick, grey |
| Esc | `interrupt` | dot, yellow |

Heavier marks are drawn over lighter ones at the same place (`Kind::rank`). Subagents' own records (`isSidechain`), the API's copies of each request and Claude Code's own notes are left out.

Permission prompts aren't in the transcript. Terminal's claudehook appends one line a prompt to `<session>.events.jsonl` in the `lbterm_summaries` folder, `{"at": <ms>, "event": "PermissionRequest", "tool": "Bash"}`, and the strip puts each on the latest call of that tool before it.

### Where Claude Code is scrolled to

Read off the screen when the conversation's rows on it change, or the input box's rules move, or the conversation itself or the pane's size does. A row changes when its words differ from what they were at the last look: Claude Code writes rows again with the same words. The rows under the conversation (2.1.312's effort line, the input box's words and the status under it) change many times a second and say nothing of where it's scrolled to, so they're not read for it, and nor is the spinner turning, inside the area in 2.1.314 (`conversation::is_spinner`: one of its glyphs at the row's start, a word ending in "…"). Until 2026-10-30 every change to the pane was read, at most every 120 ms; until 2026-10-31 each tick of real Claude Code's spinner was, 33 and 34 looks in 6 s of it alone (fake-claude drew the spinner on 2.1.312's effort line, where it cost none). Without "(ctrl+End)" on the area's last row ("Jump to bottom", or "1 new message" once a turn has ended there; until 2026-10-31 only the first counted, and the strip went to the latest where Claude Code's view stayed), it's at the latest, unless none of the rows on screen are the last screen's. Scrolled up, every row of the area that's a line of a message or a diff (your words on the band, Claude's after the dot or as a line of them, a diff's numbered line) says where the view's top could be, often several places (the same lines of a file in two diffs, "yes" sent twice); the view is where the most rows agree, give or take six rows, then where they agree most closely, then nearest where the view last was or inside the part of the prompt the header names, and each row takes its place nearest that. The middle of what they say is where it is, so one row matched by chance can't move it. A short prompt matches only itself ("message number 1" isn't "message number 10"); the header names a prompt of several lines by all of them run together. With nothing on screen to go by, the header keeps the view inside its prompt's part. On Lachlan's session the thumb was within 0.6% of the track of where Claude Code was, paging from the top to the latest in real Claude Code.

### Going to a mark

A mark goes to a line: a message's first line, an edit's header, or for a mark without words of its own (a turn's end, a folded tool call) the last line of the words just above it that Claude Code draws at that width (not a file's line past the ten rows it shows). A click on the track goes to the line at that point.

A seek works out where the view is and how many pages away the line is, and writes that many PgUps or PgDns to the pane at once (or Ctrl+Home or Ctrl+End first, when an end is nearer). It waits for the conversation to move, as long as a burst that size takes Claude Code, and then to hold still 160 ms, reads the screen and goes again from where it is; the blocks on screen say which way, should the rows be off. Once the line is on screen below the middle, one PgDn more brings it up, and once it's found the seek waits and looks once more, so a screen Claude Code hadn't finished isn't taken for where it stopped. On the fixture and on Lachlan's session every mark's line is reached in at most four bursts from either end or the middle; in real Claude Code on that session, 15 clicks of every kind landed in 0.5 to 1.7 s and stayed, and the row it landed on is marked for a moment, as the strip's prompt jump marks one (`render/prompt.rs`). Any key, wheel turn or click of yours elsewhere stops a seek where it is, so it never fights you; a drag or another click while it's going sends it on to the new place.

Claude Code can't scroll above its last compaction, so the marks there are dimmed, and a click on one says so instead of going.

## The card

Resting on a mark (within 4 px) opens a card a gutter left of the strip, level with it: each mark there, up to six, its kind and when in its colour ("You · 14:02"), and its words under it (a prompt's five lines, the rest three). A click on one goes there. A click that didn't get there says why on the card for 4 s.

### Notes

The card says, for 4 s and in place of the marks, why a click on the strip didn't get there ("Not there", in the labels' grey: Claude Code has a dialog open, it's above the last compaction, too far, not found), or that a write to the pane didn't ("Not sent", in the failed red; Lachlan's 15b of 2026-11-07), or may not have ("May not have arrived", in the same red: the connection to the mux server ended before the server answered, so it may have got there; his 4a of 2026-11-08). A write is how the window sends a pane anything but a typed key: a binding's `SendString`, a voice draft, the strip's own PgUps and PgDns, an IME's text, Lua's `pane:send_text`. On a mux pane its task hears the server's answer, and one the server refused, or lost with the connection, comes to the window holding the pane (`mux::lbterm_writes`, `lbterm_scroll_write_failed`), whose strip says why: it refused it and with what, or the connection ended before it answered and it may not have arrived (lbterm/MUX.md, "A write to a pane"). One made while the connection is down isn't a failure: it waits for the reconnect with the keys typed round it (PITFALLS 178). The strip's own seek is the exception: it steers by what each burst of keys brought, so before each burst it asks whether the pane's connection is down (`ClientPane::lbterm_link_down`), and if so sends nothing and ends with "Not sent": the mux server isn't answering and the window is connecting again (`Sought::Down`). A click's note shows on whichever pane's strip shows while it lasts; a write's on the strip of its pane's tab (the note keeps the tab), whichever of the tab's panes the strip stands by and with an overlay over it (copy mode, search), so one to the other pane of a split shows too, and one to a pane in another tab shows if that tab is switched to before it goes. While the Conductor's view is open the strip isn't drawn, and a note lapses unseen after its 4 s; a pane the mux no longer holds (its domain detached) has no strip, and the log alone has it. A seek under way in a pane whose write failed ends there, as gone, with no note of its own. `lbterm_extras::scroll_note` keeps a note's what, words, place, tab and end.

## How it's built

- **Room for it.** `render/scroll_strip.rs`'s `strip_width` (the strip and a gutter) is added to the window's right border beside the objectives strip's (`render/borders.rs`), so the panes and the mouse leave room for it, and the fancy tab bar takes it back (`fancy_tab_bar.rs`). It's at the panes' room's right edge; the objectives strip moves right by it. Its height follows the active pane's scrolled rows (`lbterm_scroll_rows_y`, the pane's rows as its text is drawn).
- **Drawing.** The strip, its thumb and marks are z-layer 38, the objectives strip's, in `over`: they never move. They're drawn as a list of shapes (render/sides.rs), and as a tab is switched from or closed the list the frame before drew, the strip as that tab showed it, goes over the next tab's in a visual of its own and fades out with that tab's panes, on `tab_switch` from the share they start from, a true cross-fade (the panes' colour under the strip is in it). A tab switched back to while it fades takes it away at once, as its panes come back whole; tabs left in a row stack oldest on top, as the panes do. Motion off, it changes at once. The card's shadow, card and words are z-layers 16 to 18, the fourth of the objectives' cards (`objectives::CARD`), in its own visual with the compositor, fading as theirs do (`Compositor::prepare_objectives`, `scroll.card`).
- **Mouse.** `lbterm_scroll_strip_mouse` runs after the objectives panel's (`mouseevent.rs`), which takes the scroll strip as a bridge to its list while the list is open. It takes every event on the strip and its card. A press on the thumb is a drag, which takes every event until it's let go, over a mark too; let go without moving 3 px, it's a click on the mark under it. Any other press or wheel turn in the window, and any key (`keyevent.rs`), stops a seek (`lbterm_scroll_cancel`).
- **Seeking** runs on the GUI thread's executor (`promise::spawn`), a burst of keys written to the pane in one go (`press`, at most 200, the escape sequences `lbterm cli send-text` would write) and then a wait for the screen to move and settle (`after_burst`, `held_still`), so nothing blocks; the transcript is read on its own thread and the windows showing it repaint when it grows. A seek that ends after a drag has moved on starts again for where the drag is. Each look is logged at debug (`WEZTERM_LOG=lbterm_gui::termwindow::render::scroll_strip=debug`).
- **The window's size.** Before each frame the window checks that its layout is for the size Windows says it is, and lays itself out again when it isn't (`lbterm_follow_window_size`, logged as a warning). A maximized window restored on its screen at start (2026-10-28) lost the size report that would have laid it out, and its panes, strips and buttons stopped about 100 px short of the right edge, the surface (which reads the size from Windows itself) drawn the whole width.
- **The state file** (`lbterm/STATE.md`): `scroll_strip`, with the strip, the track, `mode`, the pane, the thumb, every mark with its kind, the conversation's rows and the view's top, the card while open, whether a seek is under way and the note with its head (`note_head`: `Not there`, `Not sent` or `May not have arrived`); `pointer.over` names `scroll.strip`, `scroll.mark`, `scroll.thumb` and `scroll.card`; the fade is `scroll.card`, the waits `scroll.open` and `scroll.close`.

## Checking it

- `cargo test -p lbterm-extras conversation`: the transcript followed (read once shown, read on as it grows, kept while a pane names it and read on from where it stopped, one written shorter read again), read into blocks and marks (every kind, the subagents and copies left out, a failed commit, permissions matched to their call), rows at a width, a screen read (its area, scrolled or not, the header on the band), where the view is (the band, the dot, the same words twice, the header alone), bursts from the nearest end and bringing a line up the screen, the card's layout, the thumb and the track. Its `real` tests hold all of it to the real capture: the layout within a dozen rows of Claude Code's, the thumb within 5% at every screen, and every mark's line reached in at most four bursts from either end or the middle against Claude Code's own paging of the captured rows; and to 2.1.314 working (`fixtures/claude-2.1.314-working`): the spinner where it's drawn and nowhere while scrolled up, every scrolled screen read as scrolled with its header ("1 new message" included), and the spinner's ticks changing only its own row. Its `pieces_tests` hold the conversation kept in pieces (conversation/pieces.rs) to one made whole: every prefix of the made-up and the real transcripts snapshots, marks and lays out the same as if read from nothing, and every row of every screen the tests know (the real capture's and the long stand-in's) is found where a pass over every block found it. `long` is the long stand-in, made up so no real transcript is read into a test: 276 messages, 46 MB and 5,801 rows, as `perf/fake-claude.ps1 -Reads 5` draws, the thumb found at every offset; with `LBTERM_CONVERSATION_BENCH` set its `bench` says what reading it and each append cost. `cargo test -p config lbterm` for the settings. Both are in `lbterm.ps1 check`.
- `pwsh -File lbterm\perf\conversation-cost.ps1 [-Local]`: what the strip's conversation costs in a test window, on the long stand-in and a short one (lbterm/AT-REST.md, "The scroll strip and the summaries").
- `pwsh -File lbterm\perf\side-strips.ps1`: the strip fading into the next tab's at slowdown 5, its old bars and its new thumb showing the same share of the old tab as its panes in every frame, fitted to `tab_switch`; a switch back mid-fade, rapid switches, a closed tab, and motion off landing on the same strip. `cargo test -p lbterm-motion sides` and `gave_way` hold the rules to the panes'. `-Sheets` keeps each step's frames, with a contact sheet of the strips at twice their size and the switch's and the close's fade fitted to `tab_switch` (`lbterm\motion\sheet.py --crop`), in `target\motion\side-strips-<step>`.
- `pwsh -File lbterm\perf\claude-working.ps1`: against real Claude Code (the one installed), talking to a stand-in for the API on a loopback port, so no account is used and nothing leaves the PC: a long answer on the strip, the spinner alone reading the screen once at most in 6 s, PgUp moving the view, and a turn ending while scrolled up leaving it where Claude Code's is. - `pwsh -File lbterm\perf\shell-marks.ps1 [-Local]`: a shell's 50,000 rows with a prompt every 500, printed in a tab in the background, then opened: every prompt marked within 2 s, nothing fetched for it; output under it marked as it comes, a frame costing the GUI thread no more than with 100 rows; on a mux domain a new GUI attaching marking them all again.
