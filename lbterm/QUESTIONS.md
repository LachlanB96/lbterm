# Question colours

Lachlan's idea (2026-10-21): "when I hold right click on the conversation, a lesser model will show the questions it's asking me in different colours, and highlight the relevant bits in the response that are related to the question in the same colour."

While the right button's held on a Claude pane, each question in Claude's reply gets a colour, Q1 blue, Q2 green, Q3 amber, Q4 violet, Q5 teal, Q6 pink (then round again), and so does every passage of the reply that the question is about: a band over the whole bullet or paragraph, a bar down its left. A question's options (1a, 1b) take its colour too, and a passage two questions are about carries both bars. When part of a question is scrolled out of Claude Code's view, a chip at that edge of the conversation says so: "Q5 ↑" at the top for a passage above, "Q2 ↓" at the bottom for a question below. Let go and it's all gone. The wheel still scrolls the conversation meanwhile, and the colours follow it.

## How it gets there

1. **After each reply** (Terminal's `shared/claude`): `claudehook stop` hands the reply that asks something (a line outside code, quotes, tables and headings that's labelled `Q1` or ends in `?`) to `question-worker.ts` on its stdin. The worker asks Haiku (`claude -p --model haiku`, as the summary worker does) for each question's first words and quotes of the passages it's about, keeps only what's word for word in the reply, and adds it to `~/.claude/summaries/<session>.questions.json`, the session's last 20 replies that asked something. This happens before the button's pressed, so a press shows the colours at once. While Conductor's hub is alive (its `hub.json` names a live pid that beat in the last 90 s) and says `"writeQuestions": true` (since 2026-10-21), claudehook doesn't start the worker and the hub writes the same file: it finds the questions in the Haiku run that digests the step, with the worker's prompt and checks (imported from Terminal by path), and gives the sessions it doesn't digest (the Conductor's own, its ignore list) a run for the questions alone. Headless sessions get none either way.
2. **On the press** (here): Terminal's `lbterm.lua` swallows a plain right press and release in panes that take the mouse (Claude Code would paste on them), and binds those to `LbtermQuestions 'Show'` and `'Hide'`, only where `wezterm.has_action('LbtermQuestions')`, since a build without the action refuses the whole config. Every quick press is one of those: LBTerm holds a click streak at the longest any binding names for its button, a triple click at least (`config::lbterm::count_click`), so a binding made for streaks 1 to 3 takes a fourth quick press too, which until 2026-11-14 went to Claude Code and pasted. The press that shows them holds them: they go when that button's let go anywhere, when a mouse event says it's no longer down (let go outside the window), or when the window loses the keyboard (`wezterm-gui/src/termwindow/render/questions.rs`).
3. **Finding them on screen** (`lbterm-extras/src/questions.rs`): the pane holds only Claude Code's screen, the reply as it drew it: its markdown gone, its lines wrapped to the pane, two columns in. So everything is matched by its words alone (runs of letters and digits, lowercased), across rows. A table is read a cell at a time (since 2026-11-14): Claude Code wraps each cell in its own column, so its rows are read cell by cell, left to right, each cell's lines top to bottom, as the markdown has them, and a passage in a cell is coloured across its table row. Each match grows to its block: up to the row it starts on (a bullet, a number, a `Q` label, Claude Code's `●`) and down to a blank row, the next block, or a row indented less. Claude Code's input box (between the two rules at the bottom) and what's under it are left out. A passage cut off by the top or bottom edge still counts by the part that's on screen, if that part is at the edge and at least two fifths of it. The file's newest reply is laid out first, so where two claim the same rows it keeps them.
4. **Which way the rest is**: each question and passage carries where it starts in the reply, in words. What's on screen spans a range of those; a part of a question that's on screen that isn't found and lies before that range is above, after it below.

The layout is made again only when the conversation on the pane's screen (what's above Claude Code's input box, `conversation_hash`) or the session's file changes, and only while the colours show: the screen is read again only when the pane has changed, and the box and status line under the conversation changing (Claude Code's turning while it works) lay nothing out again. The bands', chips' and ticks' shapes and words are kept while they're the same (lbterm/PERF.md, "What a line costs"). Bands are drawn over the text in z-layer 23 and the chips in 24 and 25, in the compositor's `over` visual beside the prompt mark.

## While a voice draft is open

The colours also show, with no button held, on a pane on screen with a voice draft over it (lbterm/CONTROL.md, "Drafts"; the active pane's first when there are several), and each question of the newest reply that the draft answers gets a tick at the right of its first row, "✓ Q2", in its colour (`Layout::asked`, `render/draft.rs`'s `draft_for`). A held button still wins: its pane is the one shown.

The question to answer next has a red ring round it (Lachlan's idea, 2026-10-25, through Earshot's InMail; his picks 2026-10-28): the first of the newest reply's, in its order, that the draft doesn't answer yet, round its block and its options'. Once the draft answers it, its tick shows and the ring moves to the next; with every one answered there's none. Red is none of the questions' six colours, so it's the one thing on the screen saying "this one next", and it stays still. When the question is off the screen the ring goes round its marker at that edge, "Q1 ↑", with a marker of its own if nothing of the question is on the screen (which way it is comes from where it starts in the reply against what of the reply is on the screen; with nothing of the newest reply on the screen there's no ring). The extras offered after the questions (E4) count as questions to answer (`lbterm_next_question_extras`, on unless set false). A question with no number is passed by, since an answer can only name one by its number, and a picker's draft has no ring: its questions are the picker's (`Layout::next`, `lbterm-extras/src/questions.rs`).

## The questions pane and where its questions come from

Lachlan's ask (2026-11-11: "When I hold right click, a pane the size of the non-expanded tab bar pane should appear on the right side, and should show all the outstanding questions, their colour, and where they are discussed") and his picks (2026-11-14): the hold keeps the colours, and a bar beside them, the closed tab rail mirrored on the window's right, lists the questions of the held pane's conversation, newest reply first, each answered or not. It's the closed rail's size, since anything wider would hide the conversation, so a question is its colour, its label, a tick or an open mark and which way its words are, and no text ("The questions bar", below).

**One function makes the rows**: `lbterm_extras::questions::pane::rows(map, turns)`, from the session's questions file (`Map`, the worker's) and the turns read from its transcript (`Snapshot::turns`). Each `Row` has the reply it's from (its id, and its `age`, 0 for the newest), its label as asked with a stop after it dropped (`Q1`, `2`, `E4`; a question with none is named as its marker is, Q and its number or its place), its number, its colour (its place in its reply, the index the bands take, `colour`), whether it's an extra, and the last pick of his that named it; `answered()` says whether that pick settles it.

**What answers a question.** A prompt of his names a question by its number, whether he sent it when Claude was waiting (a `user` record in the transcript) or typed it while Claude worked (a `queued_command` attachment: Claude Code writes a prompt it took mid-turn only as that, never as a `user` record, PITFALLS.md 361), read line by line as a voice draft's ticks are (`drafts::pick`, `picks`, `picks_in`; `answers()` is the same reading): a number of up to three digits, the options' letters (`3deg` is 3d, 3e and 3g), a mark (`+` yes, `-` no, `?` not sure, `??` lost, `?!` shocked, `//` later), then the line's end, a space or a stop (`2.`, `2:`, `2,`, `3)`). His words after it end it, so `1+ but use 2 cores` names 1 alone; a line of nothing but picks names each (`1a 2b`, `1a, 2+`); `2-3 days`, `1.5 s`, `1/2` and `2026 was` name nothing. A pick that asks back (`?`, `??`, `?!`) leaves the question unanswered, waiting on Claude to say more and ask again; any other pick answers it, an extra declined with `-` or put off with `//` included. The last pick that names a question is the one its row keeps.

**Which reply a number is for.** The newest reply before the prompt that asks that number: a reply's labels start again at 1, so the newest is the one he's looking at, and a number it doesn't ask is still an older one's (an older reply's `3` when the newest asks only a Q1). "Before" is found by time, since the file's id is a hash of the reply's text that LBTerm doesn't keep: the reply ended in the last turn that had written something by the time the worker wrote it down (`Message::at`). The worker writes 5 to 20 s after the reply ends, and he can answer within that; the turn his answer starts is still writing after `at`, so it isn't taken for the reply's own. A turn that came and went whole inside that gap would be, and its prompt would answer nothing of that reply. A reply with no `at` is answered by nothing, and one from before the transcript's first turn by every prompt.

**A prompt in words answers a lone question, and nothing else** (Lachlan's 6a of 2026-11-14). His rule is that a question is answered once a prompt of his names it. Over the four days to 2026-11-14, about 227 of the 272 prompts that came first after a reply that asked something started with a number; of the 45 in words, some answered a lone question ("yes, do it"), and as many asked back, turned the work elsewhere or said "continue" or "pause". Stage 3 of feat/questions-pane let words answer nothing; his 6a: a reply that asks exactly one question and no extra is answered by his first prompt after it, whatever it says, since that's how he answers one question. Unless a newer reply had ended by then (his words are that one's) or the prompt names something by number (read as above: `1?` still asks back). His prompt only: a task finishing or another session's message is no answer (`Turn::his`). A reply that asks more than one, or offers an extra, still needs its numbers, and a question with no number among several can't be answered; telling an answer in words from the rest means reading the words, which the harness's `answer` entry does for its sessions.

**Where the turns come from.** The conversation's reading thread, which reads every Claude session's transcript for the scroll strip and the context list (`conversation.rs`'s `Follow` and `Conversations`), starts a `Turn` at each message to Claude: one of his prompts, with the questions it names, or another's message (a task finishing, another session's), with none. Claude Code writes a message in one of two ways, and both start a turn: a `user` record when Claude was waiting (`Conversation::user`), and, when it came while Claude was working, an `attachment` of type `queued_command` next to a `queue-operation` record, with no `user` record after it (`Conversation::attachment`: its `prompt` is the text, its `commandMode` `prompt` or `task-notification`, its `origin.kind` `human`, `peer` or `task-notification`). A queued turn splits the turn it came in, which changes nothing `rows` decides: the reply that turn goes on to write ends in the queued turn, after the queued prompt, so the prompt can't answer it. Over the 300 newest transcripts on 2026-11-14, 568 messages came queued: 276 tasks finishing, 262 other sessions' messages and 25 of his own prompts (a few more as the transcripts grew), some of them answers (`1a`, `1+` and `2+` on lines of their own). His queued prompt is a prompt's block with its mark on the scroll strip, as a typed one is (Lachlan's 7a of 2026-11-14; until then the strip drew none for it): Claude Code draws an absorbed `queued_command` in the conversation with the component it draws a typed prompt's text with (read from its bundle on 2026-11-14), so the block's lines are the screen's. Another session's queued message has no block, as before. A turn's `last_ms` is the time of its last record that changed what a snapshot shows. The turns are kept in `Chunks`, as the blocks are, so a snapshot shares them and a turn still writing copies only its own piece of 128. Nothing is read on the GUI thread for them. But a session is read only while a window says it shows it (`wezterm-gui/src/conversations.rs`, `shows` with a `Reader`), so the pane has to say so for its session while it's out.

**What turns the reading on, and what a first hold costs.** The pane reads its turns through the scroll strip's reader, and so takes its switch and its scope. `crate::conversations::folders()` gives no folders unless `lbterm_scroll_strip` or `lbterm_context` is set (to anything, `{}` included); without one, `get` and `shows` do nothing, and every row shows open for good, though the pane and its colours still show. The transcripts are read from `lbterm_scroll_strip.transcripts`, else `lbterm_context.transcripts`, else `~/.claude/projects`. And the reading thread is told of the held session only at the press (`Reader::Questions`, P1 in FLOWS.md): a session some strip already follows has its turns at once, but on the first hold of one nothing follows, every row shows open while the thread reads the whole transcript from its start (in pieces of up to 32 MB, `READ_MAX`, on the reading thread, not the GUI's), and the window gets one repaint when it's read (`repaint_showing`), when the ticks appear. The thread keeps following it after the release while a pane names it, so the next hold has them at once.

**The harness's part and LBTerm's.** ToolBelt's harness, the loop that is to take Claude Code's place (its design of 2026-11-13), gives a session's questions as structure. For a session of it these are the harness's to give, and `rows` takes them in place of today's files:

- which sessions wait on a question: its state names the blocks a session waits on, and the door's list carries each session's open ask;
- the questions: a question block's items, each with its label as asked, its text, its options by their labels and whether it's an extra. This replaces the questions file and the worker's 8 to 19 s. The passages a question is about aren't among them, so the bands for a harness session need those from somewhere else or go without;
- whether each is answered: an `answer` entry names the block it answers, with the options picked by their labels and his words. This replaces the time match above, the reading of his numbers and the choice about words.

LBTerm's stay LBTerm's, whoever gives the questions: the hold and what ends it, the pane, its motion and look, the colours (a question's by its place in its reply), finding a question and its passages on the pane's screen (`lay_out`), and which pane and tab a session is in (the `claude_session` user var). So for a harness session only `rows` changes: each item of the session's question blocks, newest block first, becomes a row, its label, extra and place from the item, and its pick from the `answer` entry naming its block, if one picks it.

## The questions bar

While the right button holds the colours on a Claude pane, a bar the closed tab rail's size lies down the window's right, the rail mirrored: its width (`config::lbterm::QUESTIONS_PANE_WIDTH`, 51pt, 68 px at 96 DPI, ring included; the same token as `RAIL_WIDTH`, the rail's default), from a gutter under the tab bar to a gutter above the window's foot, a gutter in from its right edge, its fill the panes' colour and its ring the rail's grey hairline (`palette::OUTLINE`, drawn as the rail's is, `lbterm_stroke` with its feather), and no shadow: a part of the layout, not a card over it. It lists the held conversation's questions, newest reply first, a hairline between one reply's and the next's in the grey of the rule over the rail's new-tab pad. Each question is a slot as a tab is on the rail: a pad in the question's colour with its label dark on it (the rail's pad size, `PAD_W` by `PAD_H`, widened for a label of three characters, its corners a tab pad's), an answered one's at half its colour over the bar so the ones still open stand out (Lachlan's 16a of 2026-11-14, `Row::fill`, `ANSWERED_FILL`), and under it, where a tab has its label, a tick once he's answered it or an open ring while he hasn't (`Row::answered`), with an arrow beside it while the question's own words are off the screen (below). The slots are the rail's rows: as tall as the rail's at their most (`ROW_MAX` and the line under the pad) while they all fit, shrinking together to fit more, to the rail's least (`ROW_MIN` and the line); when they don't all fit even then, the last slot says how many more there are ("+3"), the oldest replies' going first (`questions::pane::place`); none is dropped quietly. A session with no questions says "none" in its first slot; a pane with no Claude session, or one whose summaries aren't read yet (a frame or two), shows no bar. No words of the question: anything wider would hide the conversation (his 3c).

**How it went wrong first.** Stage 4 of feat/questions-pane built it as a card as tall as its rows, with a soft shadow, at the window's top right. Lachlan, seeing it on 2026-11-14: "it was supposed to be a question bar, not some crappy tooltip pane", and his rule: "we never use tooltips or panes as they look unaesthetic, we always make things look like they are a panel or bar that flows." His ask named the closed rail's size, its height as well as its width; the track's brief had kept only the width. A bar sized after another is that one's whole size and frame.

**Which way each question is** (Lachlan's E15 of 2026-11-14, for his ask's "where they are discussed"). The colours' layout gives every question in the file a place (`Layout::places`, `Where`, `lay_out` in lbterm-extras/src/questions.rs): a reply with something on the screen tells by where each question starts in it against what of it is there (on, above or below); one with nothing there is above the screen when a newer reply is on it, below when an older one is, and unknown when no reply is. The bar draws ↑ or ↓ beside the question's mark while it's above or below, nothing while it's on the screen or unknown. While the bar is out, the "Q1 ↑" markers at the conversation's top and bottom edges aren't drawn, since its arrows say the same for every question; a voice draft's red ring still gets its marker. The layout is worked out before the bar in the paint (`paint_lbterm_questions`), and the bar keeps the last places for its slide back (`PaneState::places`).

**It pushes the rail off** (Lachlan's 16 of 2026-11-14: "when the question is shown, it should push the tab bar off the screen. the conversation would shift left, but never change in size"). The bar is the rail's own size (the tab panel's `rail_width`: its width the rail's, and it comes out as far as the rail's strip, a gutter and the rail, is from the left edge), and as it comes out everything under the tab bar moves left as far: the panes, their outline, the colours and everything else `over` draws under the tab bar, the strips down the right, the objectives' and the context bar's visuals, and the tab panel itself. So the rail goes off the window's left edge, the conversation comes to rest a gutter in from it, where the rail was, at its own size, and the bar takes the room it left on the right: it covers nothing of the conversation, and no pane is resized or drawn again for it. The tab bar and the window strip above stay where they are. It's the compositor's (`Push` in compositor.rs, below); without the compositor, or with no tab panel and so no rail to push off, the bar lies over the window's right as it did before (the strips and the panes' last 24 to 28 px at 96 DPI, the last three columns of a full-width Claude pane), and a voice draft's ticks stay left of it (`render/questions.rs`, `chips_right`).

**What it costs.** The session's id is read once a press (`Shown::session`; the colours read it there too while a hold shows them, where they read the pane's user vars every frame). The rows are made again only when the summaries' generation or the conversation's revision moves (`render/questions_pane.rs`, `Made`), placed again only when they or the room change (`PaneState::placed`), the bar's quads are kept while the same rows are drawn in the same place with the same arrows, and its words while they're the same (render/kept_quads.rs, render/words.rs). Nothing is read from a file on the GUI thread: the questions file comes with the session's summaries (crate::summaries) and the turns from the conversations' reading thread, which follows the held session while a hold shows the bar (`Reader::Questions`, set as the hold starts and cleared as it ends). Until that thread has read the session every question shows open, and its first read repaints the window.

It's drawn in z-layers 111 (the fill, ring, hairlines, pads and open rings) and 112 (the labels, ticks, arrows and "+N"), which with the compositor are a visual of its own (`questions_pane`, over the bands and markers in `over`, the strips, a modal, the readout and the objectives panel's cards, and under the tab panel: COMPOSITOR.md). The state file says what a hold shows (`questions_pane`, STATE.md).

**Its motion** (since stage 5, the push since Lachlan's 16 of 2026-11-14). It slides out from under the window's right edge as the hold starts and back under it as the hold ends, and what it pushes moves with it: `questions.pane` and `questions.push` in the state file's animations, on `panel_open` out (`spring(220ms, 0)`) and `panel_close` back (150 ms ease-out), the tab panel's edge's tokens, since it's the same kind of move as the panel's own press of the panes, a panel coming out from an edge, and an exit faster than its entrance. Only visuals' offsets move: the bar's words, the conversation's text and the rail all move flat, never scaled, and no pane changes size. A release mid-slide turns it round from where it is, with its speed, and a press while it goes back brings it out again from there. Frequency: a hold is from the mouse, tens a day at most, so it may move; the colours are there at once in the press's first frame, and the bar's slide holds back nothing a click does.

How it's built (compositor.rs, `EdgeSlide` and `prepare_questions_pane`): a container at the window's origin clipped to what of the window the bar may show in (its surface's rectangle at rest, up to the window's right edge inside any border of its frame, the `edge` render/questions_pane.rs finds), holding a slider whose offset along x DWM plays from the clip's right edge, where none of the bar shows, to nothing, and the bar's surface inside that at its place at rest. So the bar comes out from under the edge it never lies over, as the context bar's parts do (`Slid`; the two can be one struct). The push (`Push`, `push_on`, `push_off`) is one more animation from the same commit: the bar's curve from where the slider is, `travel` to the left of it, set on every pushed visual at once (`Compositor::pushed`: `panes`, `focus`, `over`, `sides`, `objectives` and `panel`, root visuals whose offsets nothing else moves), so the bar and all it pushes stay the same distance apart at every frame and edges that meet stay together. `over` holds the tab bar too, which mustn't move, so while pushed it's cut at the tab bar's bottom (its clip), and the band above shows through `over_top`, a visual just over it showing `over`'s own swapchain clipped to the band, which isn't pushed: nothing is drawn twice and nothing is drawn for it. Put away, the push lets go: every offset back to nothing, still, `over` whole and `over_top` showing nothing. Over the window and not pushed: the window strip's swap and hover slots, its card, the lights (a click's ripple stays where it was pressed), the readout, the menus and the cards over the rail. Going back, the bar is drawn as it was for the session it showed (`PaneState::leaving`) while `questions_pane_showing` says its slide still shows something, and a repaint at the slide's end (`lbterm_questions_pane_wake`) puts it away: no frame is drawn per step of the slide, which DWM plays. With motion off, or without the compositor, it comes and goes at once, as before. Its surface still has the room `strip::visual_bounds` keeps round a card for a shadow, though the bar has none: nothing is drawn there.

Checking the push: the compositor test `the_questions_bar_pushes_the_rail_and_the_conversation_left_as_far_as_it_comes_out` holds every pushed visual on the bar's curve from nothing to `-travel`, the bar's own slider from `travel` to nothing, `over` cut at the band and `over_top` showing the band, unmoved, the strip's swap, hover slots, menus and lights not moved; let go 30 ms in, all of it turned round from where it was, still `travel` from the bar; put away, every offset at nothing and `over` whole; motion off, pushed at once. Checking the slide: the compositor test `the_questions_pane_slides_out_from_under_the_window_s_edge_and_turns_round_where_it_is` (compositor/imp/tests.rs) holds the slide out on `panel_open` from the clip's right edge to nothing, a release 30 ms in turning round from where it is (neither end) back to the edge, the pane showing until then, put away showing nothing, and motion off out at once. `lbterm/motion/questions-pane.ps1` captures it in a test window at a slowdown (the hold, then a release mid-slide), the contact sheet and the left edge's plot against the springs; its runs are in the script's entry in .claude/docs/perf-scripts.md.

## The weights

Lachlan's ask (2026-11-12): "when questions have answers with weights attached, the weights should be coloured. so close to 0 would be red and close to 100 would be green." His 1b of 2026-11-14 put it in the questions pane's track, since the two share this code.

**The format** (Blueprint's rules/asking.md, its third rule). When Claude has a view on a question's options, it gives each option a share of 100 for how strongly it recommends it, in brackets after the option's words and before its one-line reason: `  1a. Monthly JSONL (70): the dashboard reads a month at a time`. Shares go in steps of 10 and add up to 100 across a question's options. A question itself has none, and nor does an extra.

**What's a share** (`lbterm-extras/src/questions/weights.rs`). One rule, whoever gives the text: `share_in(words)` over an option's words, and `share(line)` over an option's line, its label first. Each gives the share and where its digits are, by char (never by byte: the cells are made a char at a time).

- The label: at the line's start after its indent, a number of up to three digits, one lowercase letter and a full stop, then a space (`1a. `, `12c. `). A `- `, `* ` or `•` bullet before it is passed over, though his rules say never one: Claude Code draws a markdown bullet as it was written, and older replies have them.
- Then the option's words, at least one letter or digit.
- Then the share: the bracketed digits the option's words end with, a space before the bracket, all before the line's first colon that a space or the line's end follows (where its reason starts), or before the line's end when it has no reason. 0 to 100, digits and nothing else in the brackets. Steps of 10 aren't checked: a 35 is coloured as a share.

So nothing else is a share: a question's line (`Q1.`, or `2.` numbered alone), an extra (`E4.`), a bracketed number in a sentence (no label), one after the colon (the reason's), one among the option's words (`1a. Keep (3) copies: ...`, since the share is what its words end with), one in a table's cell (the row starts with `│`), one in inline code (the line starts with a backtick), `(70%)`, `f(70)`, 101 or more. A time's colon (`10:30`) has no space after it, so it doesn't end the head.

**On Claude Code's screen** (`on_row(row, head)`, the Claude Code producer, to go when Claude Code's tabs do). A Claude Code pane is read a row at a time, top first, as a frame draws its rows, each row handed what the row above left it (`Head`: `Closed`, or `Open` when the row above is an option's line whose head hasn't closed). A row whose first ink is an option's label starts an option's line. Claude Code wraps a line longer than the pane onto the rows under it: a paragraph's back to its left, a list item's under its words. So with the head open, a row that's neither blank nor starts a block of its own (`starts_block`: another option, a question, a bullet, Claude Code's marks, a table's or a rule's line) is the rest of that line, whatever its indent, read as if the wrap's space and the head's words came before it. The head closes at a colon then a space, or a row that ends in a colon, since the share comes before it; the reason's rows are never read as the head's. Each share found gives its digits' columns (`OnRow`), from the glyphs' own columns, so a wide glyph before it moves its columns and not its chars. A share wrapped onto the next row is found on the row it landed on. `on_screen(screen)` runs `on_row` down a whole `Screen` above Claude Code's input box (`conversation_end`), for the tests and anything that holds a screen.

What the screen can't tell:

- A code block's lines. Claude Code draws them bare: its renderer gives the code's text, highlighted or in one colour, with no fence, bar or indent (read from its bundle on 2026-11-14, its markdown's `case"code"`). Only the colour says, and the `Screen` the colours read keeps none. So an option's line in a code block, as when a reply shows this format as an example, is read as an option's.
- An option whose label's row is above the screen: its share, wrapped onto a row on the screen, isn't found, since nothing on that row says it's an option's.
- A share broken across two rows, which only a word wider than the pane ever is, isn't placed.
- What's under a row. A row with no colon whose words end with a bracketed number is taken as ending its head there (an option with no reason), so in a line Claude Code wrapped just after a bracketed number among its words (`1b. Keep (3)`, then `copies (60): ...` on the next row) the `3` is coloured too, as well as the `60`. Rare: the wrap has to fall just there. (Stage 6's whole-screen reading joined the next row on and coloured nothing there, and coloured nothing for an option with no reason and a paragraph's row right under it, which a row-at-a-time reading now gets right.)
- Claude Code's input box. The GUI reads a row without knowing where the box is, so an option's line he typed into the box, on its second line or after (its first starts with `❯`), is coloured too.

**Where it runs, and what it costs** (lbterm/FLOWS.md, the frame flow's row 82). On the GUI thread, in the frame, where the triggers read each row (`render/triggers.rs`, `lbterm_trigger_line`), and kept with the line as the triggers' recoloured copy is, so the line quad cache keeps the coloured row by what it shows:

- Which panes are read: one with a `claude_session` user var, read from the pane the first time a window draws it (`lbterm_triggers_weighs`, a clone of its user vars) and kept in step by the user var's alert (`lbterm_triggers_claude`, from `termwindow/mod.rs`'s `SetUserVar` arm), so a frame costs a lookup a pane. A shell pane is never read.
- A row is read only when its line is new or has changed (its seqno), in the same walk of its cells as the triggers' rules: its cells are kept (each one's first char at its column) only as far as its first ink, unless that's a digit or a bullet or the row above left a head open (`weights::reads_on`), into a buffer kept from row to row. So most rows keep their leading spaces and nothing more, and the few that are read on are a few a screen. Then `on_row` on what was kept.
- What it found is kept with the line (`Weighed`: the head it was read with, the share, the head it leaves), and an unchanged line costs nothing more than the triggers' lookup it already had. A line whose row above now hands it another head (the row above changed from or to an open option's line, or its pane's session came or went) is read again and its copy made again: one more walk of it, only then.
- The share's digits and its brackets are coloured on the triggers' copy of the line (`style`: a true colour foreground on their cells, a bracket only where it's there on the row), which is drawn in its place; the pane's own cells never change, so copying the text, search and scrollback are as they were. The copy's shape hash is the line quad cache's key, so a coloured row is laid out once and its quads kept and moved like any row's.
- The head is handed down the pane's rows in the frame's order (`render/pane.rs`'s `LineRender::weights_head`, `Head::Closed` at the pane's top row), so a share on a row under its label's row is found from the frame's own walk, nothing read twice.

Nothing runs at rest, nothing per frame for an unchanged row beyond the triggers' lookup, and nothing in the mux server. Not measured: this isn't a speed branch.

The bands a hold draws (z-layers 23 to 25) go over the coloured digits as over any text, and the questions pane over the window's right as before.

**For a harness session.** ToolBelt's harness gives a reply's questions as structure: a question block's items, each option with its label and text apart (`QuestionItem`'s `options`, `{ label, text }`, @ToolBelt:session-entries). LBTerm fills a harness session's cells itself from the reply's blocks, so it runs `share_in` on each option's text as it makes that option's cells, and the digits' chars are its text's. No screen is read. The harness gives the options; finding the share in an option's words, and its colour, are LBTerm's. If the harness ever carries the share as a field of its own, that field takes `share_in`'s place.

**Its colour** (`config::lbterm::weight_colour`, its stops `WEIGHT_RED`, `WEIGHT_AMBER` and `WEIGHT_GREEN`; lbterm/DESIGN.md, "Colour"). Red `#FF5A4A` at 0, amber `#FFD426` at 50, green `#8BDB4A` at 100. Between them it runs straight from the stop below to the stop above, channel by channel as they're written, rounded, so 30 is an orange, `#FFA334`, and 70 a yellow-green, `#D1D734`. Each reads on the panes' `#121212`, red the dimmest at about 6:1. They're apart from the question colours' six: the green leans yellow (a hue of about 95°) where Q2's green and Q5's teal lean blue (147° and 180°), the amber is a full gold (about 48°) where Q3's is a soft one (40°), and the red is a red, not Q6's pink. The share's digits and its brackets are drawn in it wherever an option line shows in a Claude pane, all the time and not only while the button's held (the brackets since Lachlan's 9 of 2026-11-14; they kept the text's colour until then). `lbterm_question_weights = false` turns it off (his 10+ of 2026-11-14): no row is read for a share, and the rows coloured before are read again with no head, which takes their colour off. An extra's bracketed number (`E4. ... (60)`) isn't a share and isn't coloured. The state file says which shares each Claude pane's last frame drew (`panes[].weights`, lbterm/STATE.md).

## Settings

It needs `lbterm_summaries` (the folder the worker writes to, which `lbterm.lua` sets) and a binding. Its settings are whether a voice draft's red ring counts extras, and whether the weights are coloured.

```lua
-- The ring passes the extras (E4) by: only the questions are to answer
config.lbterm_next_question_extras = false
-- The options' shares, "(70)", keep the text's colour
config.lbterm_question_weights = false
-- In a pane that takes the mouse, a held right button (lbterm.lua does this for the ones it swallows)
{ event = { Down = { streak = 1, button = 'Right' } }, mods = 'NONE', mouse_reporting = true,
  action = wezterm.action.LbtermQuestions 'Show' },
{ event = { Up = { streak = 1, button = 'Right' } }, mods = 'NONE', mouse_reporting = true,
  action = wezterm.action.LbtermQuestions 'Hide' },
-- Or a key that turns them on and off
{ key = 'q', mods = 'CTRL|ALT', action = wezterm.action.LbtermQuestions 'Toggle' },
```

## What it can't do

- Colour what Claude Code hasn't drawn: a passage scrolled out of its view gets a chip, not a colour. No escape sequence tells a terminal where a fullscreen program is scrolled to.
- Questions asked with AskUserQuestion's picker: they aren't in the reply's text.
- A reply whose questions Haiku didn't find, or quoted wrongly: the worker drops what isn't word for word in the reply, so a wrong quote colours nothing rather than the wrong thing.

## When a hold shows nothing

lbterm/FLOWS.md, "A right hold, to the question colours", has every step from the reply to the bands, and under its "What could go" each way a hold shows nothing, with where it lives: above all, a reply's questions reach the file 8 to 19 s after the reply ends (Haiku's run, ToolBelt's to change). A fourth quick press that went to Claude Code and a passage in a table's wrapped cell that wasn't found are fixed (feat/questions-pane, stage 2). LBTerm's log says why each hold showed nothing when it's let go (`questions: shown ... ms on pane ...`, with the step that had nothing), and logs a right press in Claude Code that no binding took.
