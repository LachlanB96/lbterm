# Tab panel

A card down the left of the window, the colour of the panes and ringed with a grey hairline, holding a Claude Code status pad for each tab, with a label under it saying what the tab is for, the open tab ringed in red with a red bar beside it down the rail's edge. The moment the pointer reaches it, its ring starts to light. Rest the pointer on it and it opens into a list of the tabs (title, project, status, how long since Claude stopped), its ring lights up red-orange and casts light onto the panes, a light runs once round the ring, and the panes' cards press over to make room, each card's own outline shrinking with it. With the panel listing the tabs, the tab bar keeps only the window's buttons. Click a row (or a pad on the closed rail) to switch to that tab. It's LBTerm's own take on Terminal's TabPanel.exe, which stays as WezTerm's panel.

The look is "edge light", the fourth of four designs Lachlan was shown on 2026-10-20 (red outline, Material 3 Expressive shape morph, cards with gutters, edge light), picked for its modern feel and the way the line glows when the panel opens, casting a little light onto the black pane. Its shape follows LBTerm's design system (`lbterm/DESIGN.md`): a hairline ring, 12 px corners, and the 4 px gutter between it, the window's edges, the tab bar and the panes' cards.

## Turning it on

```lua
config.lbterm_tab_panel = {
  enabled = true,
  rail_width = '51pt',        -- the closed panel, ring included (68 px at 96 DPI)
  width = '217.5pt',          -- open (290 px at 96 DPI)
  open_delay_ms = 150,        -- the pointer rests this long before it opens (its ring lights at once)
  close_delay_ms = 300,       -- and it waits this long after the pointer leaves (its light dims at once)
  idle_full_minutes = 60,     -- the idle timer goes all red after this, like TabPanel.exe's idleFullMin
  labels = true,              -- a label under each pad: the tab's name, else its Claude session's
                              -- label from lbterm_summaries, else its title
  label_lines = 2,            -- lines a label may take (1 to 3)
  label_font_size = 7.0,      -- points, in the window frame's font
  tab_bar = false,            -- the tabs in the tab bar too; off, the bar is a slim strip the panes'
                              -- colour holding only the window's buttons, to drag the window by
  wave = {                    -- an experiment: characters washing out over the panes ahead of the
    enabled = false,          -- edge as it opens, then breaking into spray ("The wave", below)
    lead = 0.8,               -- how far ahead of the edge it sets off, 0 to 2
    reach = 1,                -- how far past the open panel it runs, in edge travels, 0 to 4
    cover = 0.85,             -- how much of the text under its crest it hides, 0 to 1
  },
}
```

The motion is `lbterm_motion.panel_open` (`spring(220ms, 0)`, the edge and the cards it presses over), `panel_close` (`150ms cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`), `panel_glow` (`spring(400ms, 0)`, the ring's light as the edge moves), `hover` (`spring(250ms, 0)`, the ring's light answering the pointer while the edge waits) and `panel_sweep` (`1400ms cubic-bezier(0.33, 0.33, 0.4, 1)`, the light running round the ring); the words fade on `pane_enter` and `pane_exit`. The wave, with it on, breaks on `panel_wash` (`450ms`, the water pulling back) and `panel_splash` (`600ms`, the spray scattering), both `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`. All of it follows `lbterm_motion.enabled` and `slowdown`. The tokens and why they are what they are: the `lbterm-motion` skill.

## The delays hold back the edge, not the answer

Every action answers at once (`lbterm/DESIGN.md`, principle 1; Lachlan's picks 1a to 4a, 2026-10-21, after the panel took 150 ms to show anything, played its open to the end when he'd already left, and showed nothing when he left and came straight back). `lbterm_panel::Hover` keeps the timing and says how lit the ring is (`Light`):

| The pointer | The edge | The light |
|---|---|---|
| reaches the closed rail | waits `open_delay_ms`, then opens | pending at once, on `hover`; the open carries it on to full on `panel_glow` |
| passes over the rail and off | stays shut | up and back, on `hover` |
| leaves the open panel | waits `close_delay_ms`, then closes | pending at once, on `hover` |
| comes back within the grace | stays open | full again, on `hover` |
| any of the above while a menu's card shows over the window, open or fading out | holds as the last frame drew it until the card has gone, then goes where the pointer has taken it (`Hover::hold`, since 2026-11-07: round the card, through its 11 px light, and through the whole card while it faded, its edge and the wave showed; the card's fill is opaque, PITFALLS 211); with motion off, let go at the paint after the card has gone (since 2026-11-08); a wave still washing as the card comes stops where it is and fades. Since 2026-11-08 (Lachlan's 7b) the rows, the marker, the pads' arcs and glints and the Conductor's outlines hold with it: a tab closed by Close tab as the card fades out, a status word changing or Copy link's glint all play once the card has gone, from where the rail showed (`lbterm_motion::rail::Hold`) | holds too |
| leaves while the edge is still opening (`panel_open`'s 220 ms) | turns round at once, from where it is | back to rest with the edge, on `panel_glow` |
| comes back onto the rail or what's left of the panel while the edge is still closing (`panel_close`'s 150 ms) | opens again at once, from where it is | full with the edge |
| clicks a pane the panel presses over | shuts at once; coming back waits on the rail as usual | to rest |

Pending is 35% of the open light and glow (`palette::PENDING`): the red starting to show in the grey ring, faintly on the panes. The light's level rides in the plan (`PanelPlan::light`), and `PanelLayers::show` moves the light alone when the edge doesn't move, so the edge's motion is never restarted. How long the edge takes on screen comes from the motion settings (`Hover::set_motion`); with motion off or no compositor there's no turning round, and the light is drawn at its level. The window strip's detail card and prompt tooltip use the same `Hover`: coming back while the card fades out (`pane_exit`) opens it again at once, but leaving while it fades in waits out its grace, since the pointer crosses the prompt row on its way down to it.

## What a row says

Nothing is polled and no file is read on LBTerm's GUI thread:

- **What the program says**, first of all. Claude Code (2.1.295 on) reports its own state with OSC 7501, the Program Status Protocol, to a terminal that answers its query, and LBTerm answers (below). The newest report from the program itself (no `id`) sets the row's pad and words: **Working**; blocked, an orange pad with **Permission**, **Question** or **Login** from its `kind`, or **Blocked**; **Done**, with a white dot on the pad while the result is unread (it arrived after the tab was last on screen); **Your turn**; or **Failed** on a red pad. When it sends a `msg` that fits whole, the message takes the status word's place, in the same colour; cut short it would say less than the word. Claude Code's are often longer than the open panel's room ("Sleeping for 4 seconds", "answer: Do you prefer red or blue? (Red · Blue)"). Its `title` replaces the tab title, and its `progress` draws a thin bar along the foot of the row while it works or waits. Its parts, Claude Code's subagents and background tasks (records with an `id`), show as "2 agents" where the age goes while it works. The age counts from the report. Watched with a real Claude Code 2.1.304 session on 2026-10-20: idle, working, working with a message, blocked on a question, done. What it leaves up from before the turn ended is over (since 2026-10-25, the index's report: a forked session on 2.1.310 said Working and "1 agent" an hour after it had handed back, with nothing running): when the hooks' newest light is a stop (anything but working) more than 2 s after the program's own working report, the hooks decide the row, and a part's record from before that stop isn't counted, unless it's one of claudehook's `bg/` records, which are sent again every minute while they run (`lbterm_panel::row`, `STALE_GRACE`). Claude Code 2.1.310's own code forgets which part records it sent when the thing it keys them by changes, without clearing them, which may be what leaves one up.
- **What only the hooks know**, over the program's word once it has handed the turn back. Claude Code reports done after any reply, but Terminal's claudehook lights a reply that asks Lachlan something in text orange (`ff8c00`), and one whose only question is the extras it offers blue (`5b7cff`). So when its report is done or your turn, an orange light makes the pad orange with **Question**, and a blue one keeps the pad blue with **Extras** and a faint orange hairline round it (`palette::extras_ring`, the waiting pad's orange 60% of the way from what's under the pad; Lachlan's ask, 2026-10-23: blue, with a subtle orange outline). Blue also wins over Claude Code's blocked on a question, for a picker whose every question is headed Extras. Working, and blocked on a permission or a login, stay the program's. Until then the rail showed Done for both (Terminal's InMail, 2026-10-21).
- **A reply gone quiet or stuck**, over the program's working. Claude Code reports working for as long as a reply runs, however long that is: on 2026-11-02 one ran 11 min 44 s generating ~110k tokens that never showed, and its pad looked like any other working one until Lachlan noticed it ten minutes in. Terminal's statusline watches for it and sets the `claude_quiet` user var through claudehook: `quiet|<since>|<stamp>` once the reply has written nothing to its transcript for 3 minutes with no tool running, `stuck|<since>|<stamp>` at 7, `none|0|<stamp>` once it isn't (`since` when the transcript was last written, in Unix milliseconds; the stamp a FILETIME, as the light's). The newest in the tab counts while the row is working and the value is newer than the tab's newest light, since a light set after it says the session has moved on before the statusline (which looks every 10 s) has cleared it. Quiet rings the green pad in amber (`palette::quiet_ring`, at a link's question ring's weight) and leaves the words Working; stuck makes the pad amber (`#FFBF00`, `RowState::Stuck`) and the words **Stuck? 8m**, its minutes counted on the open panel (Lachlan's 2a, 2026-11-02). The fix on his side is Esc in that tab, then "continue". The var repaints the window the way the light does, so a tab in the background turns too. Terminal plays Windows' error sound as one turns stuck; why 3 and 7, and how it tells a stalled reply from a tool running, are in Terminal's `shared/claude/README.md`, "A reply gone quiet".
- **State**, when a pane has no reports, from the `claude_status` user var Terminal's claudehook.exe sets in the pane (`006400` working, `ff8c00` blocked on you, `5b7cff` finished with only extras on offer, `default` stopped, each with the FILETIME the hook ran; the newest in the tab wins, as in Terminal's configs), and from the glyph Claude Code puts before its title (a braille spinner while it works, ✳ while it waits). The light wins; the glyph covers a pane without hooks and tells a finished session from an exited one.
- **Title**: the tab's own title, else the active pane's without Claude's glyph, else the program's name. Since 2026-10-25 the open row and the window strip lead with the tab's **Label** (below) instead, so a tab has one name open and closed and on the strip (Lachlan's pick, from the index's InMail: a Claude conversation has one name for him, its label); the title is the label's last fallback and the state file's `title`.
- **Waiting on its own tasks**: a program that has handed the turn back (done or idle) while parts of it still work gets a green arc turning on its pad's corner, and its row says **Waiting on N tasks** in green: it will carry on by itself when they finish, so it isn't stopped (Lachlan's pick, 2026-10-21, over dots, a ring, a colour of its own and an hourglass). Claude Code doesn't report its background tasks itself: watched on 2026-10-21, its own state went to `done` and stayed there for the 38 s a background command ran. So Terminal's statusline counts them from the transcript (a start's "running in background with ID", or a command's output ending "was moved to the background (ID: <id>)" once it passes its timeout, a command run with `!` included, since 2026-11-09; a `<task-notification>` or a stop ending it) and reports each as an OSC 7501 `working` record under `bg/<id>` through `claudehook tasks`, clearing it when it ends. Before that day a moved command counted for nothing, so a session whose `!` command had moved showed Done while its own pane said "1 shell still running". A `!` command's first 120 s, before it moves, are in no record and run no hook, so the row shows the turn's end until it moves. Without the compositor the arc is drawn still, and with it the arc holds still while the window can't be seen (lbterm/COMPOSITOR.md, "Loops"). Resting the pointer on the row lists them in a card beside it ("What's running", below).
- **Label**, under the pad on the closed rail, so the tabs can be told apart without opening the panel: the name the tab was given (Rename in the tab menu, `set-tab-title`), else the label Terminal's summary worker wrote for its Claude session, else the title. The worker (Terminal's `shared/claude/summary-worker.ts`) asks Haiku for a word or three on what the session is for, mostly from its first request, on the session's first Stop and every fourth after, and writes it to `<session>.label.txt` in the folder `lbterm_summaries` names; the session is the active pane's `claude_session` user var. LBTerm reads those files on a thread of its own and repaints when one changes (`wezterm-gui/src/summaries.rs`). Two lines of up to seven characters on the 68 px rail, broken between words, a word too long cut with an ellipsis (`text::wrap`); a wider `rail_width` fits more. Lachlan picked labels under the pads on the rail as it is (2026-10-20) over a wider rail with the title beside each pad. While the panel is open the labels fade out, since each open row's title says the same name beside it (his 12a of 2026-11-01; lbterm/NEW-TAB.md, "Landing 4"): the open panel's words cover each label's room in its pill's colour (`Layout::label_cover`, `words::label_covers`, drawn with the open parts in `chrome.open`), so they go as the words fade in on `pane_enter` and come back as the words fade out on `pane_exit`; a row the open panel raises (the open tab, the row under the pointer) has its highlight over its label and its label isn't drawn again on it. The Conductor's mode word and the foot's "+N" stay.
- **Project**: from the folder the tab's Claude session is in now, which Terminal's statusline keeps in `<session>.cwd.txt` in the `lbterm_summaries` folder; else from the active pane's working directory. The folder's name is the one every other tool on the PC gives it (Lachlan, 2026-11-02: "the name only comes from 1 place"), from the projects' `names.json` in the machine state, which their `links sync` writes: the deepest project's folder holding it names it, a worktree as `repo · tree`, a folder under a rename's new name as the project under its old one, the home folder as `~` and any other folder by its own name (`lbterm_panel::names`, since 2026-11-03; the panel worked names out from the path until then). While `names.json` can't be read the row says `names.json?`. Without OSC 7 from the shell, WezTerm finds a directory by walking the pane's processes, so it's looked up on a thread of its own, only while the panel is open, at most every 15 s. The pane's folder alone named most of Lachlan's Claude tabs "Projects" (2026-10-21): a tab opens in `Life\Projects` and its shell says so, then Claude is started in, or moves to, a project the shell never reports.
- **Cost**: what the tab's Claude conversation has cost at list price, every run of it and its subagents, at the open row's second line's end in the project's grey (`$12.34`, `$148`; `lbterm_panel::text::dollars`), the status cut short before it when the line is full (Lachlan's E6, 2026-11-07). From `<session>.usage.json` in the `lbterm_summaries` folder, which his statusline writes; a change to it alone repaints only a window whose open panel or context pill shows that session (lbterm/CONTEXT.md). The closed rail doesn't show it.
- **Waiting on another tab** (a tab link, `lbterm_links`; lbterm/LINKS.md): once its program has handed the turn back, a tab that waits on another tab's session has a purple pad, an orange ring round it while it asks Lachlan something too, and says "Waiting on Tab Wave: what it waits for"; red when the link went wrong. A line down the rail's left edge joins its pad to the tab it waits on.
- **Connected to other tabs** (a tab connection, in the same file; lbterm/LINKS.md, "Connections"): a cyan line down the rail's left edge, in a lane among the links', joins the tabs whose sessions should be talking to each other, with a mark beside each; faint with hollow marks and a light running along it while it's only suggested, steady with solid marks once it's made. The pad and words stay the tab's own.
- **Age and idle timer**: since the status last changed, for blocked and finished sessions; three bars above the pad, green, then yellow, then red over `idle_full_minutes`. Only the lit bars are drawn (his 12a of 2026-11-01, `idle::lit`): none over a tab at work or without Claude, the bottom one the moment Claude stops, all three from the timer's third step, where until 2026-11-09 the unlit ones were dim stripes (`#24242C`) over every pad. The panel repaints when an age or timer next changes, not on a clock: the idle bars' steps open or closed, the ages and the times since his last prompt (below) only while it's open, since only the open panel shows them (until 2026-10-30 every finished tab woke the closed rail each minute for its age, lbterm/AT-REST.md).
- **Since his last prompt**: how long ago Lachlan last sent the tab's Claude session a prompt, typed, a voice draft LBTerm typed into Claude Code's box, or from his phone (his ask of 2026-11-11, and his 2a of 2026-11-14 on what counts). It isn't the last key typed in the pane: a draft being typed isn't input until it's sent. So a shell tab shows none, and so does a Claude tab whose session has no time yet: never a zero. It's a different thing from the age above, which counts since the row's status last changed (a reply ending, a question asked), whatever Lachlan did, and from the Conductor's time-left estimate in the open row's corner (`text::left`), a guess at what's still to come. The time comes from `<session>.prompt.json` in the `lbterm_summaries` folder, `{ "at": <Unix ms> }`, which the statusline that writes the summaries was asked on 2026-11-14 to write whole through a rename, only when it changes, beside `usage.json` (it already finds that prompt for its turn count): LBTerm can't find it for every tab itself, since its conversation reader follows only the session a window shows (`conversations.rs`). The summaries' thread reads it with the session's other files, off the GUI thread (`lbterm_extras::summaries`, `parse_prompt`); a file not of that shape is no time, as no file is, and the log says why once, not at every read. A change to it, alone or with the session's cost, wakes as a cost does, its session on the same list (`Changed::Usage`): only the windows showing that session's dollars repaint (wezterm-gui's `summaries::repaint_for`, asking each window's `lbterm_shows_usage`: the context pill's session, or a row of the open panel), and no `generation` moves, since nothing laid out from the summaries shows it. Until the review of 2026-11-14 it woke with a variant of its own (`Changed::Prompted`, `repaint_prompted`, `lbterm_panel_shows`) that left out the pill's window; the pill's session is its window's active tab, which repaints for its status when he prompts it anyway, so that narrowing was worth about a paint a prompt, for a second walk of the tabs kept beside the first. Whatever shows it reads `Summary::last_prompt()` from the summary it already holds, and nothing else (`panel_tab`, into `Tab::prompted`): that's the seam a session of the harness that's to take Claude Code's place comes through, its time from that harness's session list in place of the file. Such a session's change has to wake the windows the same way, a `Changed::Usage` for it or its own call into `repaint_for`: a feed that only answers the read shows a stale time until something else paints. Until the statusline writes the file, its shape is what LBTerm asked for, held by stand-in files in `summaries.rs`' tests and `perf/rail-last-prompt.ps1`.
  - **Where it goes: the open row's second line, at its start, before the project** (Lachlan's 1c of 2026-11-14, by eye), in a quiet grey (`palette::PROMPTED`, `#8A8A9E`, quieter than the project's grey and never a status colour), on the left as the Conductor's guess, "~4 min left", is on the right: his "the estimate of time remaining is already on the right-hand side; the time since last input goes on the left-hand side". It says "just now" for the first minute, then "4 min", "2 h", "3 d" (his "3 min" of 2026-11-14, with no "ago", as the corner's age has none), counted down to the whole unit (`text::since`, worded as `text::left` words the guess, without its `~`, since it's known), and a time later than now (another clock a moment ahead) is just now. The project's chip, Hidden and Parked and the status follow it, a gap between; it shows only while the line keeps at least as much room again as it takes (`words::place`), so a narrow panel drops it before it cuts the project and status to nothing, and a row with one line (no room for the second) shows none. The title stays at the first line's start, where it was before the time, so no title moves when the time's words change width and every row's title lines up. The age in the corner, when the row has one, is how long since the reply ended or the question was asked; this is how long since he asked: a finished row says both, the time under its title and "3m" in the corner, read as "asked 12 minutes ago, done 3 minutes ago". Stage 2 of feat/rail-last-input put it at the first line's start before the title, where the title moved with the time's width and titles with and without a time didn't line up; his 1c moved it.
  - **Not on the closed rail**, for two reasons. Room: beside the pad the pill has 17 px either side at 96 DPI (the rail's 68 px, the pad's 22), less than "4 min" takes in the labels' font, and under it the label's two lines of seven characters are the tab's name; a third line would make every row taller by one, so labels give up their lines sooner and fewer tabs fit before "+N" (`Layout::label_lines`). Cost: its minutes would wake the closed rail once a minute for every tab prompted in the last hour, nearly always one of Lachlan's, which is what the ages did until 2026-10-30 and lbterm/AT-REST.md took out. Shown only open, its next change counts in `Changes::age`, which `Changes::next(open)` reads only for an open panel, and the closed rail at rest wakes for nothing more than before. The guess it mirrors is the open row's alone too.
  - Its words are in the row's words' look (`look::looks`), so a new minute dissolves that row's words alone, as an age's does; they change at the age's moments (`text::ago_changes_in`), never on a clock of their own, and the state file's row has them as `prompted` (lbterm/STATE.md).
- **Parked** (Lachlan's 8a, 2026-11-09; lbterm/NEW-TAB.md, "Landing 4"): a tab parked from its menu's Park tab (P) or the `LbtermPark` action has its row dimmed on the rail: its pad, lit bars, its own rings (the tab's colour, asks, quiet and extras), its spinner's arc and dot, number, label and eye-off mark each drawn 40% of the way from its pill's colour to its own, blended and drawn opaque (`palette::parked`, `words::dim_parked`; PITFALLS 248), and on the open panel its progress fills the same toward what's under them (the track as it is) and a "Parked" chip after the project's and Hidden's, in Hidden's look. With the compositor a parked row's arc doesn't turn: the turning arc is one undimmed template the compositor places on every row in the plan's `spinners`, so a parked row is left out of them (`Row::arc_turns`) and its arc drawn still and dimmed with its pad, as without the compositor, and it keeps no `rail.spinner` loop going (PITFALLS 255). The open tab's ring and bar beside its pill (the marker) aren't the row's and stay lit, and so do the Conductor's outlines round a tab it acts on or thinks about, which are the Conductor's. The flag is the mux tab's (`Tab::lbterm_parked`), read at each paint by `panel_tab`, an atomic load; on a mux domain it's the server's, and a window's park waits for the server's word (lbterm/MUX.md, "Parked tabs"). It's in both of a row's looks, so parking or unparking dissolves the row on `swap`, at once with motion off. A domain whose server is older can't park, and its tabs' menus don't offer it.

The logic is the `lbterm-panel` crate (`cargo test -p lbterm-panel`): rows, the hover timing, the layout and where every word goes.

## What's running

Resting the pointer on a row of the open panel whose Claude session still has parts going (its arc turning and "Waiting on N tasks", or "N agents" while it works) drops a card beside the row, a gutter past the open panel, listing each one (Lachlan's ask, 2026-10-24, from a comparison with oh-my-pi's Agent Hub, which lists every subagent with its job, model, age, tool calls and tokens):

```
Agent    Review the tasks card                      12m
         Edit tasks_card.rs · 31 tools · 84k tokens
Command  Run the LBTerm check                        3m
Monitor  Wait for the build queue                  40s
```

- **What each says**: its kind, its description (the Agent call's, a command's own or else its first line, the Monitor's, a Workflow run's summary: the statusline counts a Workflow run from Terminal's bf35e34 of 2026-11-09, and a kind the card doesn't know reads as Command) and how long it's been running, right-aligned. A subagent has a second line: its latest tool call in a few words (`lbterm_extras::tasks::describe`: "Read tab_panel.rs", a command's own description, "Grep owns_zindex", "Agent Review the card"), how many tool calls it has made, and its tokens: its latest request's, in and out with the cache, as Claude Code counts a subagent's. Until its transcript has said anything, its type ("Explore"). At most eight; past that the last line says how many more.
- **Where it comes from**: Terminal's statusline works out the running set from the transcript for the OSC 7501 records, and writes it to `<session>.tasks.json` in the `lbterm_summaries` folder too (its `rememberTasks`: `{ sessionId, tasks: [{ id, kind: "agent"|"command"|"monitor"|"workflow", description, startedAt, agentType?, transcript? }] }`, oldest first, written through a rename only when the list changes and removed once none run). LBTerm reads it with the session's other files, off the GUI thread (`lbterm-extras/src/summaries.rs`). A subagent's `transcript` is its own, where Claude Code 2.1.310 keeps it: `<the session's transcript folder>\<session>\subagents\agent-<id>.jsonl`. LBTerm reads it only while the card lists it, on a thread of its own (`lbterm_extras::tasks::Transcripts`), from where its last look stopped, again whenever Windows says it changed (through its folder; until 2026-10-30 the card repainted every second it was open to ask, lbterm/AT-REST.md); only its assistant lines are parsed. The card paints when what it says changes and when an age on it reads differently, and wakes only the windows it's open in. The card shows only while the tab's pane has `bg/` records (the row counts the parts), so a session that ended with tasks listed, whose file stays behind, has none.
- **When**: the pointer rests 300 ms on the row (the strip card's rest), and the card stays while it's on that row or on the card, going 200 ms after it leaves both. The card counts as the panel, so going over to it keeps the panel open. Onto another row with tasks, that row's list shows at once, beside it. The card goes with the panel, gives way to a menu, and goes when the tab's list empties. While it stays open on a tab it never gets narrower (`tasks::widen`), so its edge doesn't twitch each time a subagent's line gets shorter. The row's highlight under the pointer is what answers at once while the card waits out its rest. It fades in on `pane_enter` and out on `pane_exit`, its opacity alone, in DWM, from wherever it is, so coming back while it fades out opens it again at once; with motion off or no compositor it comes and goes at once.
- **How it's drawn**: the strip card's look (`lbterm/DESIGN.md`, "The window strip"): the surface colour, 12 px corners, a hairline at white 14%, its soft shadow; kinds at white 50%, descriptions at 85%, ages at 50%, a subagent's line at 60%, in the window frame's font. Z-layers 80 to 82. With the compositor they're a visual of their own over the panel and its glints, under the tab menu (`Target::TasksCard`, `Compositor::prepare_tasks_card`); without it they're over everything but modals, which is one reason it gives way to a menu.
- **Read only, for now**: a click on it does nothing (it's taken, so it doesn't reach the pane under it). What a click on a row could do later: on a subagent, open its transcript in a read-only pane or a split beside its parent, the way the strip's prompt list jumps to a prompt; on a command, follow its output file; and, through the parent session rather than LBTerm (Claude Code's TaskStop and SendMessage, so by way of the Conductor), stop a task or message a subagent. A subagent's own subagents aren't listed: they're in the same `subagents` folder (their `.meta.json` names the `parentAgentId`), but the session's transcript doesn't name them.

The logic is `lbterm-extras/src/tasks.rs` (`cargo test -p lbterm-extras tasks`): the file, the transcript reader, the words and the card's layout. The GUI side is `wezterm-gui/src/termwindow/render/tasks_card.rs`.

## How it's built

**Room for it.** The window keeps a strip down its left (gutter, rail) as though it were border (`render/borders.rs`), so every calculation that places panes, splits and the mouse leaves room for it, and the first card sits a gutter past it, as cards do from every edge of their room (`render/card.rs`). The fancy tab bar takes the strip back and runs the full width above the panel (`render/fancy_tab_bar.rs`). The strip is filled with the panes' colour, in `over`. Upstream's `window_content_alignment = 'Center'` (which Lachlan's `lbterm.lua` sets) worked out the leftover pixels without taking the border off, which shifted the panes' text right by half the strip and put their last columns off the window; `padding_left_top` takes it off now (`render/mod.rs`).

**Drawing.** The panel has z-layers of its own: 50 for the light, 51 for the grey hairline ring, the card inside it, the pads and their numbers, 56 for the ring lit (a red-coral-pink gradient down its sides, over the grey one while open), and 52 for what only the open panel shows (titles, ages, projects, statuses, and each row's long highlight, which starts just past the pad column so the pad shows through, the pill under it covering the rest in the same colour). Modals stay over it; the tab bar goes under it.

**The light** took three goes, and the reasons matter for anything faint drawn into a visual:

- Stacked rounded rectangles of even, faint alpha read as a flat band with a hard edge. The visual's surface is premultiplied and blended in linear light, and DWM lays it over the panes with its stored, gamma-encoded values, so over a dark pane a faint light looks far stronger than its alpha. The alpha at each distance is worked back from what should be seen instead (`palette::glow_alpha`: a Gaussian from 30% of the way to the light's colour at the ring to nothing 26 px out).
- Stacking still lost the outer light: each layer's contribution rounded to zero in the 8-bit surface before the next arrived. So every pixel is drawn once: bands along the sides and fans of segments round the corners, each quad with its own colour at each corner (`QuadImpl::set_corners`, LBTerm's one addition to `quad.rs`).
- The GPU rounds a pixel's alpha to 1/255 before blending, and near black each step showed as a ring. So a band keeps one alpha, rounded up to a step, and the falloff rides in its colour, which isn't rounded.

Every menu's light is drawn the same way since 2026-11-05 (render/menu_fade.rs, `lbterm_menu_glow`, through this file's `light_bands` and `tab_panel_bands`): `palette::menu_glow_alpha` is this falloff scaled to the strength the menus' four stacked rings had at the card's edge, out to their 11 px. The rings fell by 42 levels of red at each ring's edge at 2x; the falloff by 12 at the most (`perf/menu-keys.ps1`).

The tab bar's drawn height is what the panel sits under (the fancy bar can draw a couple of pixels taller than it says it is).

**Motion, with the compositor.** The panel is always drawn open, and DWM shows as much of it as the moment calls for, so the motion runs whatever LBTerm's GUI thread is doing (`compositor.rs`, `PanelLayers`):

| cap group: glow, cap, lit | z-layers 50, 51, 56, seen at the ring's right end | the group moves right with the edge |

The disc under the arc is drawn on the pad in the colour under it, since it looks the same at every angle | the arc turns from the moment its pad starts waiting until it stops |

The ring's two right-hand corners (the cap) ride on the edge while the body, clipped at the seam where those corners begin, grows behind them, so the ring is whole and rounded at every width. Both move on the same curve in the same commit. The two glow visuals brighten from 45% to full on `panel_glow`, and the words fade in on `pane_enter` after 60 ms, so the edge goes first.

**The panes make room without resizing.** While the panel is open, each pane's visual shows pressed into the room left of it: everything from the first card's visual to the last's mapped onto what's left once the edge has moved (`PixelRect::squeeze`), so the first card lands a gutter past the open panel and the cards stay a gutter apart. Only the visuals' offsets and clips move, on the panel's curve; the panes' content keeps its size, anchored at its top-left, so the right of each pane is cut off while the panel is open and nothing is squashed. Each card's outline is nine pieces of two template cards riding on its visual's clip (`lbterm/COMPOSITOR.md`), so the cards stay whole and rounded as they narrow, their text cut off at their own right-hand edge. No PTY is resized, so a hover never makes Claude Code redraw. A click on a pane while it's pressed over would land where the pane really is, not where it shows, so it closes the panel at once and goes no further.

**The running light** came from Lachlan asking for "a negative animation for the main pane": as the ring lights up, a light runs round the ring, brighter where it is. (With it came a grey frame round the panes that shrank with them; the design system retired it on 2026-10-20, and the panes' own card outlines shrink instead.) The light is two discs of falling-off colour (`tab_panel_disc`), a warm halo 64 px across and a near-white middle, drawn where the light starts (the top of the ring's top-left corner) and carried round by DComp: `lbterm_motion::path::along` samples `Layout::sweep_point` every 1/120 s along `panel_sweep` into straight cubics for the group's x and y offsets. It goes down the left side first, which never moves, and reaches the right side after the edge has settled there. It runs only when the panel opens, and fades out at once if the panel closes on it.

**The wave** (an experiment, Lachlan's ask of 2026-10-24: "the main pane has an ASCII wave wash over the part the tab bar will cover, just ahead of the tab bar's movement", without making the panel any slower to open; `lbterm_tab_panel.wave.enabled`, off by default). As the edge sets off, a wave of characters washes out over the panes just ahead of it, runs on past where the edge stops (`reach` times the edge's travel, 222 px at 96 DPI by default, as far as leaves its spray room inside the panes), and breaks into spray. The first version stayed inside the strip the panel covers and was gone in 120 ms, and Lachlan could barely see it (2026-10-25): "maybe the wave should come out more to the right, and also splash across the pane". This one is on screen for about 0.8 s, and he kept it as it is.

- **What it looks like** is `lbterm_panel::wave`, in the panes' font and on their rows. Each row's front sags back up to three columns along two sines so the line isn't ruled: a fleck of foam at the front (`. ' , \``), then the crest, three columns at full brightness (`~ =`), then water thinning out (`~ - .`). The colour is the ring's light at its height, whitening towards the crest. The crest hides `cover` of the text under it in the panes' colour (the foam half that), and behind it the water hides less and less, so the text shows again. Where it breaks, `wave::spray` throws droplets on past each row's front: big bright ones by the crest (`* o + O`) that hide the letter under them, and flecks further out (`. ' , :`), thinning out.
- **How it moves** is `lbterm_motion::wash`, every number one DComp animation timed from the moment the edge sets off. `path::ahead` leads the edge by `lead` times its speed times `panel_open`'s time constant (its duration over 2π), plus `beyond` times how far along the edge is, never past the end of its run and never behind the edge, in steps a cell wide held until the next. So it's off at full speed in the first frame and out on the panes as the edge settles. Within two cells of the end, about 160 ms in, the crest breaks. The water pulls back three cells as it fades (`panel_wash`, 450 ms), and the spray lights up on `copy_in` and scatters six cells on as it fades (`panel_splash`, 600 ms).
- **How it's drawn.** The water and the spray are each drawn once, the water with its front where its run ends and the spray from just short of there, into surfaces of their own (z-layers 83 and 84, `PanelPart::Wave` and `Spray`). They take a frame only when their look changes (their sizes, the cells, the colours, `cover`: `WavePlan::look`, against the look the compositor says they hold), so they cost the paint nothing the rest of the time. The compositor's `WaveLayers` shows them under the rest of the panel in a group clipped to the room they wash over. The clip's left edge rides on the ring's right edge on `panel_open`, the same motion as the cap, in the same commit, so they never part.
- **The edge is untouched**, so the panel opens exactly as fast as without it: from the pointer arriving to the edge moving, 68.5 ms without the wave and 69.8 ms with it over eight interleaved runs each (2026-10-25; a run's own noise is about ±15 ms), and the edge still fits `panel_open` to under a pixel RMS.
- Turned round as it opens, the wave and its spray stop on the steps they're on and fade on `pane_exit`. So they do when a menu's card comes over the window while they wash (since 2026-11-07: a right click on a pad within the wave's ~0.8 s washed it round the card and through it as it faded), and the panel doesn't open under a card at all (below). Closing, nothing runs. With motion off, or no compositor, there's no wave.
- `pwsh -File lbterm\motion\panel-demo.ps1 -Wave` records it (`-WaveLead`, `-WaveReach`, `-WaveCover`; `-Slowdown 3` to see it step, `-Live` to try it by hand).

**The open tab.** Its pill on the rail has a ring of the accent red round it, a hairline drawn by `lbterm_stroke`, and a short bar of the same red runs down the rail's left edge beside it, inside the panel's ring. Lachlan picked the two together (2026-10-20) when the faint red pill alone was too hard to see, over a stronger red pill and a white ring. With labels on, every pill is as wide as the closed rail lets it be, 42 px on the 68 px rail: nothing per tab may show right of the seam, where the ring's right-hand corners (the cap) begin. Open, a highlighted row's pill grows into its long highlight, drawn whole in the words layer, and the ring goes round all of it; the row's pad, timer, number and label are drawn again on top, so nothing of the pill underneath shows through.

**The rows move on their own** (built 2026-10-21, `PanelLayers::sync_rows`, `sync_marker`, `sync_progress`), each from wherever it is when a new change comes, so nothing restarts or jumps:

- A row is followed by its tab, not its place. A row whose slot changes (a tab closed above it, moved, dragged past) slides there on `pane_layout`. A new tab's row grows in: its clip opens from the top on `pane_layout` as the rows below make room, and it fades in on `pane_enter`. A closed tab's row keeps the old surface's picture and fades where it was on `pane_exit`. A row whose look changes dissolves from its old look. Each row has a face on each of the rows' two surfaces; the other surface holds the frame before the change, so its face keeps the old look while the front's comes in with the new one, and the old goes once the new is three quarters in (`swap`, or `hover` for a hover alone). The surfaces are clear where nothing's drawn, so the old fading over the new hid only what went: the open tab's fill showed at once (2026-10-29). Fading both at once would dim what the looks share, a quarter of the way to what's under them halfway; holding the old until the new is three quarters in keeps that under 5%. The faces sit inside the row's visuals, so a dissolving row slides with its old look, and a row that turns back mid-dissolve turns round from where it is. While anything still shows the kept surfaces, a row still dissolving carries on through a further change, and one they hold as it shows now dissolves from them, without swapping the surfaces, so nothing fading is cut short.
- Each row's look is two hashes of what it shows, never how far its progress is (`lbterm_panel::look::looks`, which names every field of a row so a new one has to be put in one or the other): the closed rail's (its pad's state, idle bars, rings and unread dot, its number and label, whether it's the open tab, how it stands on a link; the tab's colour is added to it) and the open panel's words' (its title, project, status, message, agents, age and level, and which bars it has). While the panel is closed only the first counts, so a new status, message or age on a row the rail shows the same doesn't dissolve it; open, either does (`RowPlan::restyles`). When it changes the old picture is kept: the rows draw into one surface and show the other, and a row that changed gets a ghost visual showing the old surface, faded out over the new on `swap` (on `hover` when only its hover changed). The two surfaces swap each time any row changes.
- What the rows are made of is made once and kept (lbterm/PERF.md, "What the cards cost"): the rows' pills and pads, their parts on the open panel, the bars, the ring, its glow and lit gradient, the marker, the running light's discs and every word are kept as quads by what each is made from (`render/kept_quads.rs`, `render/words.rs`), and a frame that changes none of it copies them in, where each frame made them all again (2.2 ms of a 3.7 ms paint pass with fifteen tabs, 0.9 after).
- The marker (the open tab's ring and bar) glides from the old row to the new on `focus_move`; when its row moves, it moves with it on `pane_layout`.
- A progress bar's visual is clipped to how far along it is, and the clip's right edge glides on `pane_layout`.
- A row being dragged along the panel (`tab_mouse.rs`) is lifted out of the order: it's drawn with a soft shadow, its visual moved over the others, and it sits under the pointer at once on every move, while the rows it passes slide out of its way. Let go, it slides into its slot.
- A pad glints, the copy flash's white (`copy_in`, `copy_out`) round it, when a program types into its tab through `lbterm cli` and when the tab is renamed (`lbterm_panel_glint`). The session handler (the mux server's, or a window's own socket's) raises the `lbterm_typed` user var on the pane when the connection typing never said who it is: a window sends `SetClientId` as it attaches and the cli never does (`mux::lbterm_typed`), so the Conductor's, Relay's and the Claude project's typing shows and a GUI's own doesn't. It's a notification, not a stored var: nothing in Lua sees it.
- The window resizing, or motion off, puts every row, the marker and the bars where they belong at once.

**One outline.** Lachlan asked (2026-10-20) for the outline to be one solid colour at rest and to light up only when the panel is in use. The ring is a grey hairline (`palette::OUTLINE`) at rest, drawn by the routine every outline uses (`lbterm_stroke` in `render/card.rs`: a stroke soft a pixel either side, its colour free to change down its length), with the design system's 12 px corners. Opening fades the gradient ring and its glow in over the grey one on `panel_glow`. Until the design system it was a 3 px stroke with 18 px corners and up to 10 px of gap, paired with the grey frame round the panes, which needed Lachlan's `lbterm.lua` to pad the panes 12pt.

**The tab bar steps aside.** With the panel on, the fancy tab bar draws no tabs, no new-tab button and no status, only the window's buttons, on a strip the panes' colour that the window is dragged by (`fancy_tab_bar.rs`, `config::lbterm::hides_tabs`). `lbterm_tab_panel.tab_bar = true` brings them back. The retro tab bar doesn't know the setting.

**The mouse on a row** (lbterm/TAB-MOUSE.md has each in full). A left click on a row or its pad switches to that tab, a drag moves it or tears it off, and a right click opens the tab menu, which reads its facts from the rail as the last paint drew it (`tab_panel_menu_rail`: each row's status, unread dot, agents and tasks without its tab link, its pad's colour, its label and its objectives' mark), so Wait on…'s page shows each tab after a dot in its pad's colour and Close finished tabs closes only what the rail shows finished (lbterm/NEW-TAB.md, section 7; landing 2, 2026-11-05). With the panel off no row is drawn, so neither of those two shows, even in the tab bar's menu. A middle click on a row closes its tab at once, without asking (Lachlan's 10a, 2026-11-05): kept to reopen with its close marks written, as Close tab is, so Ctrl+Shift+T brings it back; it does nothing on the Conductor's pad or the + pad (`tab_panel_mouse`'s `MousePress::Middle` arm).

**The + pad at the foot** (lbterm/NEW-TAB.md, landing 1, built 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04; `lbterm_tab_panel.new_tab`, on by default). The rail's foot slot, `Layout::foot`, the mirror of the Conductor's head: a 42 by 30 px pill with a + in it under a hairline rule, which counts as a slot in `row_height`, so it never falls off. When the rows don't fit at their least with two lines of label, they give up a line, then the other (`label_line`, `label_lines`), before any tab leaves the rail; past 28 px rows the rule says "+N" for the tabs with no room (`hidden`). Its zone runs across the closed rail, and while the window is maximized on to the window's left edge and its last pixel row, so a flick into the screen's corner lands on it (`foot_zone`). Under the pointer it lights on `button_hover`, from a template kept in z-layer 96 (render/new_tab.rs), nothing of it drawn at rest. A left click opens the picker beside the rail and a right click the + menu (DESIGN.md, "The + pad", and TAB-MOUSE.md have both); while either is open the panel holds where it is, closed or open.

**The Starting row** (built 2026-11-03): every tab asked for in the window (a pick, a shell, Reopen, Shell in its folder (Duplicate tab until landing 2), Lua's `window:lbterm_expect_tab`) puts a row at the end of the tabs the next frame, under `new_tab::STARTING_KEY`: a dim pad with the white waiting arc turning on its corner (its template in `START_SPIN_Z`, 98, turned by DWM, `rail.starting` among the state file's loops), "Starting…" on the open panel, and in the state file a rail row with `pending`. The first tab the window didn't have takes its place, its row dissolving out of the Starting row's (`RowPlan::was`); one opened behind glints. With no tab in 20 s, or `lbterm_expect_tab(nil)`, it folds and the strip says "Nothing opened for …". Its wait is `lbterm_extras::newtab::starting_now`, which never says the time's up before it is, however late it's asked.

**Reopen** (built 2026-11-04): a tab closed by hand is kept, the last ten in memory, newest first (`TermWindow::lbterm_new_tab_closing`, from `lbterm_mark_tab` and `lbterm_mark_pane` in termwindow/last_tab.rs, where close marks are written): its row's label, its panes' Claude sessions, its folder (its session's cwd.txt, else its pane's) and the picker's name for its project. The + menu lists up to five, and `LbtermNewTab 'Reopen'` (Ctrl+Shift+T) takes the newest: each fires `lbterm-reopen-tab` and, with no handler returning false, opens the default program in its folder behind a Starting row.

**Without the compositor** (`lbterm_compositor = false`, or OpenGL), the panel is drawn at its size of the moment, over the panes, with no motion and no running light (DWM carries it).

## OSC 7501

The Program Status Protocol (Mitchell Hashimoto's; spec at superlogical.com/rex/docs/build/program-status) lets a program tell the terminal what it's doing. LBTerm speaks it in every pane, whatever the config, because a program only reports once a terminal has answered its query:

- `wezterm-escape-parser/src/program_status.rs` parses a report or the query `ESC ] 7501 ; ? ST`, with every limit in the spec: keys and values from their character sets, a bad pair skipped, a bad `state` or `id` dropping the report, an oversized sequence or bad base64 dropping all of it.
- `term/src/program_status.rs` keeps up to 256 records per pane, by `id`, evicting the least recently updated. `state=clear` removes a record and its children; OSC 133 A (a new prompt, or N, which starts one too) drops the working and blocked ones, so a crashed program doesn't stay "Working"; RIS drops them all. `app` is inherited from the nearest record above. A report saying again what its record says (claudehook's minute resend of a running task's `bg/` record) changes nothing: the record keeps the time it last changed, which the age counts from, and only moves up the eviction order.
- `performer.rs` answers the query with `ESC ] 7501 ; ? ESC \`, which comes back through ConPTY's input intact (checked 2026-10-20), and raises `Alert::ProgramStatus` on every change. The window holding the pane repaints on it when its tab's row would show anything else (`lbterm_panel::claude::programs_move_row`, against the row as the last paint made it), and another window only while its projects card is open (`render/tab_panel.rs`, `lbterm_program_status`): a part's words, or a subagent paused, which its row counts the same, paint nothing (lbterm/MUX.md, "What a status report costs").
- `pane:get_program_status()` in Lua returns the records as tables (`id`, `state`, `kind`, `progress`, `app`, `title`, `msg`, `updated` in Unix seconds, when the record last changed, not when it was last said), so Terminal's `lbterm.lua` can use them too.
- The stale-stop rule (a Working record older than the hooks' last stop is a leftover, unless it's a `bg/` record) reads the same `updated`, so a record said again word for word after the hooks' stop stays a leftover until the hooks light working; before 2026-10-30 a repeat made it current again. Only Claude Code's reporter remounting repeats its own records, and the hooks light working whenever it really works again.

`cargo test -p wezterm-escape-parser program_status` and `cargo test -p wezterm-term program_status` cover the parse and the records.

## The Conductor

The Conductor (Lachlan's `~\AI\Conductor`) is one Claude Code session that watches and directs the others. LBTerm shows it on the rail (picked 2026-10-21; the look is `lbterm/DESIGN.md`'s, under "The tab panel"). Since 2026-10-24 it runs in the background in its seat, and LBTerm draws its view (below, "The Conductor's view"):

```lua
rawset(config, 'lbterm_conductor', {
  state_file = [[...\Megaproject\conductor\button.json]],  -- { "state": "off"|"on", "mode": "read"|"most"|"all", "asks": 1, "sessionId": ..., "tooltip": ..., "hidden": [session id, ...] }
  hub_pipe = [[\\.\pipe\conductor-hub]],                     -- unset: no outlines on the tabs
  seat_pipe = [[\\.\pipe\conductor-seat]],                   -- unset: no view, and the tab holding its session stands for it
  glyph = '\u{f075a}',                                       -- on its pad; a note by default
  remote_glyph = '\u{f0003}',                                -- on the pad's corner while state is "remote"
  menu = {
    { label = 'Accept most', when = { state = { 'on', 'remote' } }, checked = { mode = 'most' },
      wait = true, action = wezterm.action_callback(function(window, pane) ... end) },
  },
})
```

- **Its pad** is a slot above the tabs (`Layout::head`), a tab's height, with a hairline rule under it; the tabs go down a slot. Its state file is read on a thread of its own when Windows says it changed (`state_files.rs`, through its folder; it looked every 250 ms until 2026-10-30), and no file is off. On the open panel its row says its label (else "Conductor") over the file's `tooltip`. With tab connections suggested that have a tab on this rail, a small cyan badge on the pad's top left corner counts them, none at 0 (lbterm/LINKS.md, "Connections"; the remote control's mark has the top right). Read only, its outline is grey, and the spectrum while the pointer is on the pad. Accept most and Accept all (Lachlan's 7a of 2026-11-01, landing 4: NEW-TAB.md): a still hairline in the mode's colour, its label's (yellow, red), and the spectrum only while the Conductor acts on a session, any, here or not (its hub's `conductor.focus` naming one `acting`, with the least lap and the linger a tab's outline keeps: `Focus::acting`, `conductor_hub::acting`, asked once a paint of the rail), or the pointer is on the pad; thinking about a session doesn't light it, and read only takes no notice of acting (`conductor::outline`: `none`, `grey`, `mode` or `spectrum`, the state file's `outline`). The still hairline stays drawn in the body under the spectrum's ring (`conductor::hairline`), so the ring fades in over it and back out to it. A right click on it drops its menu from where it was clicked, and so does a left click while there's no tab of its to open (`render/conductor.rs`): the items whose `when` the file matches (a field the file hasn't got is `''`, but `state` is `off`), ticked where `checked` does. An item with `wait = true` keeps the menu open once picked, its arc turning where its tick goes and the others dimmed and out of reach, until the state file says anything new or 45 s pass (`conductor::answered`). A new `updatedAt` counts, so a rewrite in the same words is an answer too (since 2026-10-21 Conductor's `on`, `remote`, `off` and modes rewrite the file when it's so already, and `restart` does). A ticked item doesn't wait (`conductor::waits`): it asks for what's so already, the mode the Conductor is in, so nothing would change, and the menu doesn't hold for the rewrite; Esc or a click away stops waiting. The arc is drawn into a template (z-layer 76) that a visual in the compositor's `top`, over the menu, shows turning a turn every 1.4 s (`Compositor::prepare_menu_spinner`); without the compositor it's drawn over the menu, still.
- **The sessions hidden from it** are the state file's `hidden`, the Claude session ids Lachlan hid from the Conductor (`conductor hide`; lbterm/NEW-TAB.md, section 8), read with the rest of the file on the same thread into `Said::hidden` (`conductor::Hidden`): no list there (a Conductor from before 2026-11-02, or no file) is `None`, and then the tab menu offers no Hide from Conductor. **The seat's pipe** (`seat_client.rs`; Conductor's `SEAT.md`). LBTerm keeps one connection open on a thread of its own (`lbterm-seat`), from the rail's first paint with `seat_pipe` set, the view open or not (`seat.show` comes on it): its first line is `{"type":"seat.subscribe","id":…,"client":"lbterm","pid":…,"version":…}` (`lbterm-try` in a try window), and from then on it keeps the last `seat.view`, the conversation (`seat.items` replaced by id, `reset` on subscribe; `seat.delta` the reply so far), and what the seat answered each command. A change repaints the windows whose view is open and no others (`lbterm_extras::seat::Views`): nothing else shows what the seat says, so with the view closed everywhere its lines paint nothing (until 2026-10-30 every line repainted every window, a frame for each new view and ten a second while the Conductor wrote a reply; lbterm/AT-REST.md). When the connection closes or can't open, the view says the seat isn't running, a reply being written is dropped, and the thread tries again after 2 s, doubling to 30 s, as the hub's client does, and at once when a command is sent or the config reloads; with no seat in the config it sleeps until a reload names one. Commands go on the same connection (`SEAT.md` lets a subscriber send them), written in the order they're pressed, every one with an id (`lbterm-<pid>-<n>`) and `by: "lbterm"`, and each waits for its reply until its own deadline without holding the rest: a control up to 2 minutes (the seat answers once the Conductor's Claude has started or ended), an answer 30 s (once it's typed and the question has gone), the rest 15 s. The pipe is opened for overlapped I/O, so one thread writes a command while its read waits, and every wait has an end: the seat's next line, a command or a reload, the next reply's deadline, or the seat hanging up (`lbterm_extras::seat::link`). A reply that never comes is said once its wait is over, and nothing is left waiting on it; until 2026-10-30 each command had a connection and two threads of its own, and the one reading its reply stayed blocked, with its handle, for as long as a seat that never answered kept the connection. They are: `seat.say` (`from: "lbterm"`), `seat.allow`, `seat.deny` (`why` empty), `seat.interrupt`, `seat.control`, `seat.answer` (`picks` one list per question, from 1, `other` his words per question, `text` a text question's) and `seat.rsi` (`send`, `dismiss`, `rate` with `useful` or `not`). The logic, tested without a window, is `lbterm_extras::seat` (`protocol`, `text`, `input`, `layout`).
